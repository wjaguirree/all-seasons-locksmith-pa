Good to know

Common questions.

What are the signs that my ignition switch needs replacement instead of a key copy?

If the key only works sometimes, sticks in one position, won't return from START, or gets trapped in the cylinder, the ignition hardware may be worn out. A copied key can make the symptom worse, but a replacement is usually needed when the cylinder's pins, wafers, or housing are damaged.

Can you re-key a new ignition to my existing car key?

Often, yes. If the vehicle allows it and the old cylinder isn't too damaged, we can pin the replacement ignition to match your current key. That saves you from carrying a separate key for the ignition. Some models need extra steps because of anti-theft electronics or immobilizer programming.

What vehicles usually need ignition replacement work?

We see this on a wide range of vehicles, including Toyota, Honda, Ford, Chevrolet, Nissan, Hyundai, Kia, Jeep, RAM, Subaru, and BMW. The exact method depends on the steering column design, whether the key is transponder or smart/proximity, and how the ignition housing is built.

What should I have ready before I call for ignition replacement in Millersburg?

Have the vehicle year, make, model, and where it's parked, such as the town square, Market Street, or near the Millersburg Ferry. If you still have the key, keep it handy. If the car is locked and the key is inside, mention that too so the right tools can be brought.

Why is ignition replacement sometimes harder than it looks?

The cylinder may be tied to the steering lock, shifter interlock, or immobilizer system. Some ignitions can be decoded and matched with pinning kits, while others need VIN-based key origination or programming. Broken wafers, seized parts, and damaged trim can all slow the job down if the column has already worn out.
