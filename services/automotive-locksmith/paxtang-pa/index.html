Good to know

Common questions.

Can you make a car key if I lost every copy?

Yes, in many cases we can. The process depends on the vehicle and key type. We may use the VIN, decode the door or ignition, or program a new transponder or smart key directly to the vehicle. All-key-lost jobs are more involved than duplication, especially on newer high-security systems.

What's the difference between a transponder key and a regular car key?

A regular key mainly has a cut blade that matches the lock. A transponder key also contains a chip that has to be recognized by the immobilizer before the engine will start. If the cut is right but the chip isn't programmed, the door may turn and the car still won't run.

My key fob stopped working on Derry Street. Is it the battery or programming?

It could be either. We usually check the simplest things first: battery condition, button response, and whether the fob still communicates with the vehicle. If the battery is good, the problem may be lost programming, a damaged chip, or a vehicle-side issue. Testing tells us which path makes sense.

Do you repair ignitions, or only replace them?

We do both. If the cylinder is sticking, worn, or not reading the key correctly, repair may be enough. If the internal parts are damaged beyond reliable use, replacement is the better fix. The decision comes from testing the key, the lock cylinder, and how the ignition behaves when turned.

What should I have ready for a car key replacement in Paxtang?

Have the vehicle accessible, plus proof that you own it if that's needed for the job. If you still have one working key or fob, bring it. That can make duplication or programming simpler. It also helps to know the exact make, model, and year, since Toyota, Honda, Ford, BMW, and other brands can use very different systems.
