What new fob creation actually involves

A new fob starts with the hardware, not the software. When the original is gone or dead, we first identify the vehicle by make, model, year, and the exact key system it uses: a simple remote fob, a transponder key with a chip, a smart/proximity fob, or a high-security setup with a laser-cut or sidewinder blade. On many Toyota, Honda, Ford, Chevrolet, Nissan, Hyundai, Kia, Jeep, RAM, Subaru, or BMW systems, the shell, battery contacts, chip type, and emergency blade style all have to match before programming even begins.

From there, we verify ownership, read the vehicle's immobilizer requirements, and decide whether the fob can be cloned, decoded, or must be programmed by VIN-based key origination. Some cars allow non-destructive programming through the onboard diagnostics port; others need a key programmer, pinning data, or a security code pulled from the vehicle. If the lock cylinders are worn, the door won't decode cleanly, or the car has been rekeyed before, that can change the route. The goal is a fob that locks, unlocks, starts the car, and fits the hardware the way it should.

Good to know

Common questions.

Can you make a new car fob if I have no working fob at all?

Yes. That is the core of new fob creation. We identify the vehicle, verify ownership, and then source the correct fob or transponder blank for the exact immobilizer system. Some vehicles allow onboard programming; others need key origination through VIN data, a programmer, or security-code retrieval.

What do you need from me to create a replacement fob?

Have the vehicle itself available, plus the VIN if you can get it. Any broken shell, old blade, or dead fob pieces help too. The make, model, and year matter, but the security system does as well, since two cars of the same year can use different transponders, chips, or smart fobs.

How do you know whether my car needs a transponder fob or a smart/proximity fob?

We check the vehicle's lock and ignition system, then match it to the correct key family. A transponder key usually has a chip in the head or blade area. A smart/proximity fob lets the car detect the key nearby and often uses push-button start. The wrong format will not program correctly.

What makes new fob programming harder on some vehicles?

Security level is the biggest factor. Some models require VIN-based key origination, pin codes, or a specific programmer to access the immobilizer. Worn door locks, previous rekeying, or a damaged ignition can also complicate decoding. High-security systems from brands like Medeco or Mul-T-Lock are a different world than standard automotive remotes.
