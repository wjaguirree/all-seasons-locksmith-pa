Good to know

Common questions.

Can you make a new car key if I lost every copy?

Yes. If there is no working key left, we can usually still originate a replacement by using the vehicle's VIN, lock code information, or direct decoding at the car. After the blade is cut, we program the chip or remote so the immobilizer recognizes it. Some vehicles require extra steps if the ignition or doors were replaced.

What kinds of car keys can you replace on-site?

We replace many transponder keys, laser-cut or sidewinder keys, basic metal keys, and many smart/proximity fobs. That covers a lot of Toyota, Honda, Ford, Chevrolet, Nissan, Hyundai, Kia, Jeep, RAM, Subaru, and some BMW models. The exact process depends on the key style, security system, and whether the vehicle uses a programmed chip.

Do you need my car to be at a dealership for programming?

Usually, no. A mobile automotive locksmith can cut and program keys where the car is parked, which is helpful if the vehicle is stuck at home, in an older driveway, or near a trailhead. The main exceptions are certain high-security or encrypted systems that need specialized procedures or additional verification.

What should I have ready before you come out?

Have the vehicle registration and a photo ID ready so we can verify you are authorized to have the key made. If you know the year, make, model, and whether you have a transponder, remote, or smart key, that helps too. If the car is near Route 34, Route 94, or by the old paper mills, tell us exactly where it's parked.

Why is one car key replacement harder than another?

Difficulty depends on the vehicle's security system, whether all keys are lost, and whether the locks match the ignition. A simple mechanical key is faster than a smart key with encryption. Weak batteries, damaged ignition cylinders, swapped parts, or a worn lock can all change the process and may require decoding or additional programming steps.
