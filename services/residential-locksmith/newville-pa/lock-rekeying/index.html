What lock rekeying actually involves

When a house in Newville changes hands, the lock does not know who is supposed to come and go. That is the security issue with leaving it alone: old keys can still work, and you do not always know how many copies are out there from previous owners, contractors, or family members. Rekeying fixes that without replacing the whole lock. The cylinder is opened, the old key pins are removed, and new pins are matched to a fresh key so the hardware only accepts the new pattern.

On historic Main Street homes and farmhouses along Route 641 or Route 233, the process starts with checking the lock brand and condition. Kwikset, Schlage, Baldwin, and Yale rekey differently, and worn hardware or damaged plugs can make a simple rekey turn into a repair. For many entry locks, a tech can use a pinning kit, decode the existing key, or key the cylinders alike so one key works on several doors. If a lock is high-security, such as Medeco or Mul-T-Lock, the procedure may involve specialty key control and different tooling. Good candidates are solid, clean locks with matching cylinders; tougher jobs are corroded exterior locks, mismatched deadbolts, or older hardware that has been painted over or forced.

Before the appointment, it helps to gather every key you want matched, know which doors should work on one key, and point out any locks that stick or feel loose. That makes the rekey smoother and helps the technician decide whether the existing hardware is worth keeping.

Good to know

Common questions.

What does rekeying a lock actually change?

Rekeying changes the pins inside the cylinder so the old key no longer lines up. The knob, deadbolt, and exterior hardware stay on the door if they are in good shape. It is a good choice when the security concern is key control, not broken hardware.

Is rekeying better than replacing the whole lock?

If the lock body works well, rekeying is usually the cleaner option because it keeps the existing hardware and just changes who can open it. Replacement makes more sense when the lock is worn out, damaged, or the grade and style no longer fit the door.

Can one key work on several doors in my Newville house?

Often yes. A locksmith can key multiple cylinders alike so one key opens the front door, side door, and other matching locks. It works best when the locks are the same brand or compatible models, and when the cylinders are in decent condition.

What should I have ready before a rekeying visit?

Gather every key you still have, even partial copies, and decide which doors should be matched. It also helps to note any sticky deadbolts, loose knobs, or painted-over hardware. Those details tell the locksmith whether the lock can be rekeyed cleanly or needs repair first.

Can you rekey older locks on historic Main Street homes?

Usually, yes, but older hardware can be more involved. Paint buildup, worn plugs, or nonstandard parts may slow the process. A locksmith will check the brand, test the cylinder, and decide whether the lock can be re-pinned or whether the internal parts are too worn to keep.
