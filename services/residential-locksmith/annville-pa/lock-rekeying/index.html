What lock rekeying actually involves

When a homeowner asks whether to repair, replace, or rekey a lock, the first thing I look at is the hardware itself. A solid deadbolt from Kwikset, Schlage, Baldwin, or Yale can often be rekeyed and put back to work if the body, latch, and strike are sound. That means changing the pins inside the cylinder so old keys stop working, while keeping the exterior hardware in place. If the lock is bent, corroded, stripped, or not meeting the door correctly, replacement may make more sense. On older Annville homes near downtown Main Street, that decision often comes down to how well the door and frame have aged, not just the key.

The rekeying process starts with identifying the lock type and checking whether it uses a standard pin tumbler cylinder, a high-security cylinder such as Medeco or Mul-T-Lock, or a smart/proximity setup that needs a different plan. For standard residential locks, I remove the cylinder, read the current pin stack, and repin it to a new key with a pinning kit and the correct key cuts. If the key is lost after a move, VIN-based key origination may apply to some vehicles on the same service call, but home rekeying is its own job. The work is easier when all exterior doors use matching brands and common keyways. It gets more complicated with mismatched hardware, damaged keys, or doors that have shifted on Route 422 and Route 934 area homes over time.

For student housing near Lebanon Valley College, rekeying is often the smarter choice after a roommate change, lease turnover, or key handoff that got out of control. If the door already has a decent deadbolt and the knob or lever is still tight, there is no reason to buy new hardware just to regain control of access. I can usually rekey the lock, check the strike, and confirm that the new key works smoothly from the outside and inside before I leave.

Common questions.

Should I rekey my lock or replace it after moving into a house in Annville?

If the deadbolt or knob is solid, the door closes well, and the hardware is not damaged, rekeying is usually the first thing to consider. Replacement makes more sense when the lock is worn, mismatched, loose, or not fitting the door correctly. In older Annville homes, the condition of the door and frame matters as much as the lock brand.

What actually changes during a lock rekey?

The outer hardware stays in place, but the pins inside the cylinder are changed so the old key no longer works. I match the pin stack to a new key, test the turn, and check for smooth operation. On standard residential locks from brands like Kwikset, Schlage, Baldwin, or Yale, this is a precise mechanical change, not a cosmetic one.

Can every residential lock be rekeyed?

Most standard pin-tumbler locks can be rekeyed, but not every lock should be. Some high-security systems like Medeco or Mul-T-Lock need specific parts or procedures, and some damaged cylinders are better replaced. If the keyway is worn, the plug is damaged, or the lock has been forced, rekeying may not be the right fix.

How do I know if my lock is the problem or my door is the problem?

A lock can feel stiff even when the cylinder is fine if the door has shifted, the strike is out of line, or the latch is scraping the frame. I check the key turn, bolt movement, and how the door sits before I decide on rekeying or replacement. On older downtown Annville homes, alignment issues are common.
