What deadbolt installation actually involves

If you need a deadbolt in Jonestown, the real question is not just whether the lock turns, but whether the door, frame, and strike plate are all working as a unit. A dealership or big-box aisle can hand you a box, but it cannot check how your older front door fits in the jamb, whether the latch bore is centered, or whether the screws are biting into wood that has seen years of seasonal movement. That matters in the borough core and in the older homes near the Swatara Creek, where doors often need more than a simple swap. We install grade-rated deadbolts from names like Kwikset, Schlage, Baldwin, Yale, Medeco, and Mul-T-Lock, matching the hardware to the door material and the level of security you want.

The process starts with measuring the existing prep and inspecting the door edge, strike area, and existing hardware. On a clean retrofit, we can usually use the current bore and set the bolt to match the door swing. If the door was never drilled for a deadbolt, we lay out the new hole, drill from both sides to keep the face clean, then mortise the edge for the bolt body and cut the strike into the frame. Harder jobs usually involve warped doors, soft jamb wood, metal-clad doors, or old hardware that left oversized holes behind. We bring the tools to pin, align, and reinforce the installation so the bolt throws smoothly and the strike holds under force.

What helps most is knowing the door material, whether it is wood or metal, and whether you want keyed entry, keyed alike with other locks, or a higher-security cylinder. If you already have the hardware, we can work with that if it fits the door correctly; if not, we can explain which ANSI Grade 1, 2, or 3 option makes sense before anything gets cut.

Good to know

Common questions.

Should I buy a deadbolt at the store first, or have it installed with the door in mind?

Either can work, but the door should come first. A deadbolt has to match the door thickness, backset, bore size, and frame alignment. If the door is older or has been patched before, a box-store lock may not fit cleanly. We look at the door, then choose the right hardware and prep method.

What makes deadbolt installation harder on older homes in Jonestown?

Older homes often have settled frames, uneven latch alignment, and previous repairs that left oversized or off-center holes. In Jonestown, that can show up in the borough core and near the Swatara Creek. The job may need fresh drilling, a new mortise, or strike reinforcement so the bolt actually locks under pressure.

Can you install a high-security deadbolt like Medeco or Mul-T-Lock?

Yes. Those systems use higher-security cylinders and controlled keyways, so the install has to be precise. We check the door prep, confirm the backset and bore, and make sure the cylinder and trim sit correctly. High-security hardware helps most when the strike plate and frame are also reinforced.

What should I have ready before a deadbolt installation visit?

Know which door needs the bolt, whether it is wood or metal, and whether you want the new lock keyed separately or keyed alike with other doors. If you already have hardware, keep the box and all parts together. Clear the area around the door so the frame, edge, and interior side are easy to inspect.

Do you only install deadbolts for front doors in Jonestown?

Front doors are the most common, but side entries, garage entry doors, and rear doors can also benefit from a proper deadbolt. Around Jonestown, especially with homes close to Route 72 or the I-81/I-78 area, it usually makes sense to look at every primary exterior door and match the hardware to each opening.
