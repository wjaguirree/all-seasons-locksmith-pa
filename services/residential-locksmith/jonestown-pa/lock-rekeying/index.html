What lock rekeying actually involves

Sometimes the problem shows up the moment the key turns in a new door and you realize you do not know who else still has a copy. In Jonestown, that often happens in the borough core or in one of the older homes near the Swatara Creek, where previous owners, relatives, contractors, or even a well-meaning neighbor may still have access. Lock rekeying fixes that without replacing the whole lock: the cylinder stays in place, but the pins are reset so the old key stops working and a new key becomes the only key that opens it.

The job starts with identifying the lock brand and cylinder type, whether it is Kwikset, Schlage, Baldwin, Yale, or a higher-security setup like Medeco or Mul-T-Lock. From there, the technician removes the cylinder, works through the plug with a pinning kit, and matches fresh bottom and top pins to a new key. If the lock is worn, keyed on multiple doors, or built with restricted keyways, the process can take more care. Rekeying is often ideal for residential doors with ANSI Grade 1, 2, or 3 hardware because it restores control without disturbing the door, strike, or trim. On a house near Route 72 or just off the I-81/I-78 interchange, it is also a practical way to bring every exterior door back to one key.

Before the appointment, it helps to know which doors should match and which ones should stay separate. A good rekey can often be finished in one visit, but the exact work depends on the lock's condition, keyway, and whether any deadbolts have been previously altered.

Good to know

Common questions.

What happens during lock rekeying?

The locksmith removes the cylinder, takes out the old pins, and installs a new pin combination that matches a fresh key. The outside hardware stays on the door. After reassembly, the old key should no longer work, and the new key becomes the only one that opens that lock.

Is rekeying better than replacing the whole lock?

If the lock body, deadbolt, and trim are in good shape, rekeying is usually the cleaner choice. It changes access without disturbing the door. Replacement makes more sense when the hardware is damaged, badly worn, or too limited to match the doors you want keyed alike.

Can you rekey older home locks in Jonestown?

Yes. Older homes in the borough often have mixed hardware, and rekeying is a practical way to bring those doors under one key. The tech will check the lock brand, cylinder wear, and whether the pins and plug are still in serviceable condition before starting.

Can one key open all my doors after rekeying?

Often, yes. A locksmith can rekey multiple compatible locks to the same key, including front, side, and back doors. That depends on the brands and keyways involved. Some high-security locks, restricted keys, or mismatched cylinders may need a different setup.

What should I have ready before a rekey appointment near Route 72 or the I-81/I-78 interchange?

Have the doors you want changed identified and make sure they are accessible. If possible, gather every existing key you have, even the ones you think no longer matter. It also helps to know whether you want one key for all exterior doors or separate keys for certain entries.
