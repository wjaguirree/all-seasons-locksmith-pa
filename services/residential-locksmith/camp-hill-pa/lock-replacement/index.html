What lock replacement actually involves

Replacing a lock is not the same as buying the first shiny knob off a shelf. In Camp Hill, especially in the established homes around the school neighborhoods, Willow Park area, and downtown Camp Hill, the better choice is often to match the hardware to the door, the latch prep, and the way the family actually uses the entrance. A dealership or big-box store can hand you a box. We look at whether the door needs a fresh deadbolt, a new keyed knob, a smart/proximity lock, or a stronger upgrade in an ANSI Grade 1, 2, or 3 setup from brands like Kwikset, Schlage, Baldwin, Yale, Medeco, or Mul-T-Lock.

The process starts with checking the existing bore, backset, strike, and door condition. On older doors, worn screw holes, swollen wood, or an off-center strike can make a simple swap fail unless the new hardware is fitted correctly. From there, the old lock comes off, the replacement is matched to the door thickness and keyway, and the latch and strike are tested so the bolt throws cleanly without binding. If the homeowner wants one key to work several doors, we can rekey the new lock on site with pinning kits. If the upgrade involves smart hardware, we confirm battery access, app setup, and whether the door already supports the needed prep before anything is assembled.

What makes one replacement easy and another fussy usually comes down to the door itself: paint buildup, old patchwork around the bore, misaligned hinges, or a previous installer who forced the hardware. Having the current key, the door open for inspection, and a sense of whether you want keyed entry, deadbolt-only security, or a smart lock helps the job move along the right way.

Good to know

Common questions.

Should I replace my lock or just rekey it?

Rekeying changes who can open the lock; replacement changes the hardware itself. If the lock body is worn, the key is hard to turn, the finish is failing, or the door hardware no longer lines up, replacement is the better move. If the lock is solid and you only need new keys after a move, rekeying may be enough.

What kinds of locks do you install on Camp Hill homes?

We replace residential entry hardware from brands like Kwikset, Schlage, Baldwin, Yale, Medeco, and Mul-T-Lock, including deadbolts, keyed knobs, lever sets, and smart/proximity locks. We also match the new lock to the door thickness, bore size, and latch style so it works cleanly instead of just looking new.

Can you make my front door, side door, and back door use the same key?

Yes, in many cases. That depends on whether the new locks share a compatible keyway and whether the cylinders can be re-pinned together. We use pinning kits to set the locks to one key when the hardware allows it. If the locks are mixed brands or different security levels, we'll explain the options clearly.

What makes a lock replacement harder on older houses in Camp Hill?

Older doors can have enlarged bores, split wood around the latch area, paint buildup, or strike plates that no longer match the bolt throw. Hinges that have sagged can also throw the alignment off. In those cases, the job is less about swapping parts and more about fitting the new hardware to the door correctly.

Do you replace smart locks or just mechanical ones?

We replace both. For smart or proximity locks, we check door prep, battery access, and whether the model fits the existing bore and backset before installation. We also verify that the lock opens and closes smoothly by hand before pairing or programming it, because a smart lock still has to fit the door like a proper lock first.
