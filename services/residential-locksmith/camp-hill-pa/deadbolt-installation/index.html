What deadbolt installation actually involves

On a deadbolt installation call in Camp Hill, the first thing a technician checks is the door itself. A good deadbolt can only work well if the door, frame, and strike area are solid enough to hold the lock under force. That means looking at door thickness, bore size, backset, edge alignment, and whether the latch line actually meets the strike without rubbing. In the borough's established homes, especially around the school neighborhoods and the Willow Park area, older wood doors often need a careful reset before any new hardware goes on.

From there, the job turns into fit-and-function work. We measure for a standard single-cylinder deadbolt or a keyed deadbolt that matches the rest of the house, then choose hardware that fits the door and the level of security you want. Kwikset, Schlage, Baldwin, Yale, Medeco, and Mul-T-Lock all have different bodies, cylinders, and key systems. On some doors, a Grade 1 or Grade 2 deadbolt makes sense; on others, the issue is making the strike plate stronger with longer screws and a cleaner mortise so the bolt actually catches the frame. If the door is already prepped, the work is usually straightforward. If the bore is off, the trim is tight, or the frame has shifted, the process takes more fitting, testing, and adjustment before the lock operates smoothly.

After installation, the last step is always a full test from both sides: key turn, bolt throw, door closure, and re-entry with the door locked and unlocked several times. That is how you know the deadbolt is securing the house instead of just sitting in the door.

Good to know

Common questions.

What does a locksmith check first before installing a deadbolt?

The first check is the door and frame, not the lock. A technician looks at door thickness, bore size, backset, edge alignment, and the strike area in the jamb. If the door is warped, the hole is off-center, or the frame is soft, the deadbolt may need fitting work before it can secure the door properly.

Can you install a deadbolt on an older Camp Hill front door?

Usually, yes, but older doors often need more prep. In Camp Hill's established homes, the door may need a fresh bore, a corrected strike, or reinforcement where the bolt lands. If the wood is worn or the frame has shifted, the installation can still be done, but the hardware has to be matched to the door conditions.

What type of deadbolt is best for a residential front door?

For most homes, a single-cylinder deadbolt with ANSI Grade 1 or Grade 2 hardware is a solid choice. Brand options like Kwikset, Schlage, Baldwin, Yale, Medeco, and Mul-T-Lock all have different levels of security and key systems. The best pick depends on the door, the frame, and whether you want standard keying or higher-security protection.

Do you need to replace the whole lock when adding a deadbolt?

Not always. Some doors only need a new deadbolt added above an existing knob or lever lock. Other jobs are better served by rekeying the current hardware so one key works both locks. The technician will check whether the existing hardware is in good shape, whether the new deadbolt will fit cleanly, and whether the door needs reinforcement.

How do I know if my deadbolt is installed correctly?

A correct install lets the key turn smoothly, throws the bolt fully into the strike, and closes without dragging or forcing the door. The bolt should land squarely in the frame, not just scrape the edge of the strike plate. If the door needs shoulder pressure to lock, the strike or bore likely needs adjustment.
