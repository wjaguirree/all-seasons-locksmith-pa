What lock rekeying actually involves

When a homeowner in Lebanon asks whether a lock should be repaired, rekeyed, or replaced, the first thing I look at is the hardware itself. On older houses in Downtown Lebanon, North Lebanon, or near Cumberland Street, it is common to find original deadbolts and knob locks that still work, but no longer match the security needs of the home. Rekeying keeps the existing lock body, changes the pin setup inside the cylinder, and makes old keys useless. That is often the right move after a move, when a tenant leaves, or when a key has been shared too widely.

The process is straightforward but not guesswork. I inspect the cylinder, test how the key turns, and check whether the plug, springs, and pins are worn or damaged. If the lock is in decent shape, I remove the cylinder, decode the existing keyway, and repin it with a new key pattern using a pinning kit. If it is a Kwikset, Schlage, Yale, or similar residential lock, that usually goes smoothly. If the lock is sticky, corroded, or an older original on a Lebanon home near the Farmers Market or the Cornwall Road area, repair may come first, or replacement may make more sense. The goal is simple: keep what still has life, change what controls access, and leave the door working the way it should.

One reason rekeying stays practical in Lebanon is the mix of older housing stock and active family homes near places like Coleman Memorial Park and the Lebanon VA Medical Center. A house may have sturdy exterior hardware, but keys may have been passed between contractors, relatives, or former occupants. Rekeying resets access without changing the look of the door or disturbing good-grade hardware, including many ANSI Grade 1, 2, or 3 residential locks.

What makes the job easier is having every door key you want to keep, plus a clear list of which locks should match one another. What slows things down is a misaligned strike plate, a damaged latch, or a cylinder that has been forced. In those cases, I may need to repair the lock before rekeying it, or recommend a full replacement if the mechanism is too worn to hold a reliable key pattern.

Good to know

Common questions.

What is the difference between rekeying a lock and replacing it?

Rekeying keeps the existing lock hardware and changes the internal pin arrangement so old keys no longer work. Replacement means removing the lock and installing new hardware. If the lock body is solid and the problem is access control, rekeying is usually the better first question. If the mechanism is worn, damaged, or poorly fitted, replacement may be the smarter fix.

Can you rekey the locks on an older Lebanon house with original hardware?

Often, yes. Many older homes in Downtown Lebanon and nearby areas still have serviceable residential locks that can be repinned. The key question is condition: if the cylinder is worn, corroded, or binding, the lock may need repair first or replacement instead. I check the mechanism before deciding, because older hardware can be worth keeping when it still performs well.

Which lock brands can usually be rekeyed?

Common residential brands like Kwikset, Schlage, Baldwin, and Yale are often good candidates for rekeying. Some high-security systems, such as Medeco or Mul-T-Lock, can also be serviced, but they may require special tooling and control procedures. The exact model matters more than the brand name, so I inspect the keyway and cylinder before starting.

What should I have ready before a rekeying appointment?

Have the door accessible, gather every key you still want to keep, and know which doors you want matched together. It also helps to mention any sticking, loose handles, or keys that already turn rough. If the house is in Lebanon near Cumberland Street, Route 72, or the Cornwall Road area, telling me which entry doors are involved helps streamline the work.

Can rekeying help after moving into a home near the Lebanon VA Medical Center or the Farmers Market?

Yes. After a move, rekeying is one of the most practical ways to reset access without changing hardware that still works. That is especially useful in neighborhoods with older homes and shared driveways or side entries. I can make the front, back, and interior entries match where appropriate, or keep certain doors keyed separately if you prefer different access for different areas.
