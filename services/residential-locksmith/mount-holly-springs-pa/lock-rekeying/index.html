What lock rekeying actually involves

When we arrive for a rekeying call in Mount Holly Springs, the first thing we check is the hardware that is already on the door. On the older mill-town homes along Route 34, that often means a mix of Kwikset or Schlage knobs, deadbolts that have seen a few prior tenants, and strike plates that may not line up as cleanly as they should. We confirm that the lock body is worth rekeying, test the keyway, and look for worn pins, bent keys, or a cylinder that has been drilled, forced, or repinned before. If the hardware is sound, we remove the cylinder, read the pinning pattern, and reset it so a new key works while the old ones do not.

The process is precise work, not guesswork. A locksmith uses pinning kits, follower tools, and key gauges to match the new key cut to the lock's chambers. On some homes near South Mountain or the old paper mills, exterior doors have older cylinders or mixed hardware grades, which can make the job slower if parts no longer match standard sizes. If a customer wants several locks keyed alike, we map each cylinder so one key opens them all. If the home has high-security hardware like Medeco or Mul-T-Lock, or a smart/proximity system, the process changes and may require manufacturer-specific parts or programming instead of a simple pin change. Having every door key, any spare, and a clear idea of which locks should match helps the work go smoothly, usually in one visit.

Good to know

Common questions.

What does a locksmith check first before rekeying a lock in Mount Holly Springs?

The first check is the lock itself: the cylinder, keyway, latch action, and whether the hardware is worth rekeying. On older Mount Holly Springs homes, we also look for wear, previous drilling, misalignment, and whether the existing brand uses standard pins or something more specialized. If the lock is sound, we can usually reset the keying without replacing the whole unit.

Can you rekey all the locks in my house to one key?

Yes, if the locks are compatible. We compare the cylinders, confirm they're usable, and then pin them to the same key pattern. That's common for front doors, back doors, and garage entries. Some mixed hardware, older cylinders, or high-security systems may need different parts or a different approach before they can be keyed alike.

Do I need to replace the hardware to change who has access?

Usually not. Rekeying changes the pins inside the existing lock so old keys stop working and a new key works instead. That's often the best choice for a move, a rental turnover, or a lost key situation. If the lock is badly worn, damaged, or not worth repairing, then replacement may make more sense.

What makes rekeying harder on older mill-town homes in Mount Holly Springs?

Older homes can have mismatched hardware, worn cylinders, and doors that don't line up perfectly. Near the mill-town blocks or Route 34, we sometimes find older Schlage, Kwikset, Baldwin, or other mixed locksets that have been serviced before. That can affect how cleanly the cylinder comes apart and whether the lock needs adjustment after rekeying.

Can you rekey a lock if I only have one working key?

Often, yes. A working key helps because it gives us the cut pattern to pin the lock to. If the key is worn, bent, or not the original, we'll inspect the cylinder carefully before starting. For some high-security or vehicle-style systems, extra identification or manufacturer-specific parts may be needed, but most residential rekeying is straightforward.
