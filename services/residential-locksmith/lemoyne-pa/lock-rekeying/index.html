What lock rekeying actually involves

You usually don't think about who still has a key until the moment it matters: a move-in day in Lemoyne, a contractor hands back a ring you don't recognize, or a former key turns up in the wrong place. That's when rekeying makes sense. Instead of replacing the whole lock, we keep the existing hardware and change the key that operates it. On many homes near Negley Park, along Market Street, and up the hillside blocks, that means taking apart the cylinder, removing the old pins, and setting new pin combinations to match a fresh key.

The work starts with identifying the lock brand and keyway, whether it's Kwikset, Schlage, Baldwin, Yale, or a higher-security cylinder like Medeco or Mul-T-Lock. From there, the technician checks whether the lock is in decent shape, whether the deadbolt and latch function smoothly, and whether any worn parts will make rekeying a bad fit. Older hillside homes can bring extra variables: stiff hardware, mismatched locks on the same door, or cylinders that have been handled before. Rekeying is usually straightforward when the cylinders are standard and the internal parts are not badly worn; it gets more involved when a home has multiple lock brands, smart/proximity hardware, or damaged keyways. If you're scheduling the job, have the doors accessible and gather every key you want removed from circulation.

After the new pins are set, the technician tests each cylinder, checks the key for smooth insertion and turning, then verifies that every intended lock works on the same key if that's part of the plan. The goal is simple: keep the hardware you already have, change the access, and make sure the new key works the way it should the first time it's used.

Good to know

Common questions.

What does rekeying a lock actually change?

Rekeying changes the pins inside the cylinder so the old key no longer works and a new key does. The lock body, deadbolt, and exterior hardware stay in place. It's the right move when the hardware is fine but you want to control who can get in after a move, a key loss, or a change in access.

Can all of my house locks be set to one key?

Often, yes, if the locks use compatible cylinders and keyways. A technician checks the brands and sizes first. Kwikset and Schlage are common examples, but mixed hardware can complicate the job. If one lock is worn or a different security level, it may need repair or replacement before everything can be matched.

How is rekeying different from replacing a lock?

Rekeying keeps the existing hardware and changes the internal pinning. Replacement means installing a whole new lock. Rekeying is usually the better option when the latch, deadbolt, and finish still work well. If the mechanism is damaged, corroded, or out of alignment, replacement may make more sense.

What should I have ready before a rekeying appointment in Lemoyne?

Have every door you want serviced accessible, plus all existing keys you can find. It also helps to know which doors should open with the same key, especially in older hillside homes with front, back, and side entries. If you're near Market Street or Negley Park, clear access to the locks saves time once the technician arrives.

Can rekeying help if I moved into an older Lemoyne house?

Yes. Older homes often have locks that have been keyed and rekeyed more than once, especially on the hillside blocks. Rekeying lets you reset access without changing the character or fit of the existing hardware. The technician will check whether the cylinders are standard, worn, or already modified before starting the work.
