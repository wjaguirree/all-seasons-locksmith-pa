What smart lock installation actually involves

Smart lock installation is not just swapping a deadbolt for a keypad. On an older Hershey house in Downtown Hershey, Deer Run, Parkview, or Spring Creek, the door often tells the story before the lock does: slightly out-of-square frames, worn strike plates, paint buildup around the bore hole, or a latch that never quite lined up. On newer homes off Cocoa Avenue, the work usually shifts toward clean fitment, battery access, and app setup. In both cases, we start by checking the door thickness, backset, bore size, and whether the existing hardware is a standard deadbolt or a multi-point setup. A smart lock only works well when the mechanical side is right first.

From there, the installation process depends on the lock and the door. We may re-drill or adjust the strike, install a Grade 1, 2, or 3 deadbolt body, pair the keypad or app, and verify that the latch throws smoothly without binding. For some models from Kwikset, Schlage, Yale, or Baldwin, programming is straightforward; others need careful account setup, firmware updates, or calibration so the bolt doesn't fight the door. If the property is a rental turnover near Hersheypark Drive or a new build close to Route 39, the customer can help by having the old hardware available and knowing whether they want entry codes, phone access, or both. The harder jobs are usually the ones where the door is shifted, the frame is tired, or the existing prep was done for a different style of lock.

Good to know

Common questions.

Can any door in my Hershey home take a smart lock?

Not every door is a clean fit. We check bore size, backset, door thickness, and whether the jamb and strike are aligned. Older doors in Downtown Hershey or Spring Creek may need adjustment before a smart lock will latch smoothly. Newer doors often fit standard smart deadbolts with less modification.

What's the difference between installing a smart lock on an older house and a new build?

Older houses often need mechanical correction first: enlarged or worn holes, misaligned strikes, paint buildup, or a door that sags. New builds usually have cleaner prep, so the focus moves to programming, Wi-Fi or Bluetooth pairing, and making sure the bolt throw matches the door and frame exactly.

Do you set up keypad codes and phone app access too?

Yes. A proper smart lock install includes more than mounting the hardware. We set entry codes, connect the lock to the app when the model supports it, confirm user access, and test lock and unlock functions from both the keypad and the interior side. That helps avoid callbacks for basic setup problems.

What kinds of smart locks do you install?

We install keypad and app-controlled smart locks from common residential brands like Kwikset, Schlage, Baldwin, and Yale. Depending on the door and the homeowner's goals, that can include keypad deadbolts, smart deadbolts, and models with app control, code management, or proximity features. The right model depends on the door prep and usage.

Can you help with smart lock installs for rentals near Hersheypark or homes off Route 39?

Yes. Rental turnovers and newer homes off Cocoa Avenue are both common smart-lock jobs because they benefit from code-based access. We can set up the lock so the owner can change codes, manage entry without spare keys, and keep the hardware working correctly even when the door gets heavy daily use.
