What lock rekeying actually involves

When you do not rekey after a move, you are not just living with old hardware — you are leaving old keys in circulation. In Hershey, that matters in a town built on turnover: rental resets off Cocoa Avenue, new-build homes, and houses near Downtown Hershey or Spring Creek can all have keys floating through contractors, cleaners, previous tenants, and anyone else who had lawful access before you took possession. Lock rekeying changes the key that operates the cylinder while keeping the lock itself in place, so the outside looks the same but the old key no longer works.

The job starts with identifying the lock brand and cylinder type. Kwikset, Schlage, Yale, Baldwin, and similar residential locks are often rekeyed by removing the cylinder, sorting the top and bottom pins, and pinning the plug to a new key with a keying kit. With some higher-security hardware, including Medeco or Mul-T-Lock, the process can involve special keyways, control cards, or a different handling procedure. We check each door for wear, latch alignment, and whether the key should operate smoothly before we finish. If a deadbolt drags or the door is out of square, rekeying alone will not fix that, so it is better to catch it while the lock is apart.

For customers near Route 422, Route 39, or Hersheypark Drive, the most useful thing you can have ready is the number of doors to be changed and any existing keys that still work. If you have a smart or high-security key, bring what you were given at closing or move-in, because that helps match the correct key profile and avoid delays.

Good to know

Common questions.

What does lock rekeying actually change?

Rekeying changes the pins inside the cylinder so the old key no longer works and a new key does. The lock body, deadbolt, and trim stay in place. It is the right move after a move, after a tenant turns over a rental, or when you want to control access without replacing good hardware.

Can you rekey most residential locks in Hershey?

Usually, yes, if the lock is a standard residential cylinder from brands like Kwikset, Schlage, Baldwin, or Yale. Some high-security products, such as Medeco or Mul-T-Lock, need special handling. If the lock is worn, damaged, or misaligned, we look at whether rekeying alone will still leave you with a smooth turn.

What should I have ready before a rekey appointment?

Have the doors you want changed identified, along with any working keys you were given at closing or move-in. If the home has multiple exterior doors or matching locks, say so up front. That helps us plan the keying so one key can work where it should and not where it should not.

Is rekeying a good idea for rental turnovers off Cocoa Avenue?

Yes. Rental turnovers are one of the clearest times to rekey because you do not know how many copies of the old key exist. In Hershey, where rentals, shift workers, and seasonal traffic overlap, rekeying after each change in occupancy is the cleanest way to reset access without replacing every lock.

How does rekeying work on a smart or high-security lock?

With some smart or high-security locks, the mechanical side can still be rekeyed, but the process may involve a special cylinder, a control key, or manufacturer-specific parts. We first identify the exact model, then determine whether it can be changed with pinning or whether a different core is needed. The key is matching the hardware to the right procedure.
