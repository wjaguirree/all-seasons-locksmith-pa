What door lock repair actually involves

When we arrive for a door lock repair in Paxtang, the first thing we check is not the lock itself but how the door and frame are meeting. A deadbolt can look fine and still fail if the strike plate is low, the hinges have dropped, or the latch is rubbing on the edge of the door. On the tree-lined blocks near Paxtang Park and along Derry Street, that kind of wear shows up a lot in older residential doors, especially where seasonal swelling and settling have changed the alignment.

From there, we test the key, cylinder, latch, and bolt travel by hand. If the hardware is salvageable, we may tighten hinges, reset the strike, adjust the latch, or re-pin or lubricate the cylinder so it turns cleanly again. If the issue is inside the lock body, we look for worn springs, bent tailpieces, damaged pins, or a miscut key that is fighting the mechanism. Some jobs are simple alignment work; others involve replacing a Kwikset, Schlage, Yale, Baldwin, or high-security lock component with matching parts and correct ANSI-grade hardware. The easier the door closes squarely and the key is already in decent shape, the faster the repair usually goes. A cracked frame, painted-over hardware, or a lock that has been forced can make the process more involved, but the goal stays the same: get the door latching smoothly again without unnecessary replacement.

For a lot of Paxtang homes, the repair starts with careful adjustment, not guesswork. That keeps the door operating the way it should and helps avoid repeat sticking or binding later.

Good to know

Common questions.

What do you check first when repairing a door lock in Paxtang?

The first check is usually door alignment. If the door is sagging, rubbing, or hitting the strike plate wrong, even a good lock will act up. We test the latch, deadbolt throw, hinges, and strike position before deciding whether the fix is adjustment, internal repair, or replacement.

Can a sticking lock be repaired, or does it always need to be replaced?

Often it can be repaired. A sticking lock may only need a tighter strike plate, hinge adjustment, cylinder cleaning, or new internal parts like springs or pins. If the body is cracked, the keyway is badly worn, or the mechanism has been forced, replacement can make more sense.

What kinds of residential locks do you work on?

We repair many common residential locks, including Kwikset, Schlage, Baldwin, and Yale, plus some high-security options like Medeco and Mul-T-Lock. We also handle standard deadbolts, knob and lever locks, and smart or keyless hardware when the issue is mechanical, electrical, or alignment-related.

Why does my deadbolt work when the door is open but not when it's shut?

That usually means the bolt and strike plate are no longer lined up. When the door is open, the bolt throws freely. When it's closed, the bolt may hit the edge of the strike or rub the frame. We check hinge movement, plate position, and door settlement to correct that.
