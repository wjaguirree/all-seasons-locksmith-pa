What lock replacement actually involves

Lock replacement looks simple from the outside, but the work changes a lot between a historic King Street house and a student rental near Shippensburg University. On older doors, the first job is checking the bore, strike, backset, and whether the door has shifted over time. A worn knobset may have loose spindle parts, stripped screw holes, or a latch that no longer meets the strike cleanly. On newer doors, especially rental units that see constant turnover, the concern is often whether the existing hardware is still the right grade for the door and whether all exterior entries match the same keying plan.

We start by identifying the lock brand and style — Kwikset, Schlage, Baldwin, Yale, Medeco, or Mul-T-Lock — then confirm whether replacement is better than rekeying. If the hardware is damaged, out of alignment, or missing key security features, full replacement makes more sense. The process usually includes removing the interior trim, checking the latch and deadbolt throw, measuring the door prep, and fitting new hardware that matches the door thickness and bore. For higher-security setups, we also look at ANSI Grade 1, 2, or 3 hardware and whether the customer wants standard keyed entry, a deadbolt upgrade, or keyed-alike sets for multiple doors. Small details matter: a warped frame, painted-over strike, or mismatched latch can turn a quick swap into a careful adjustment job.

For Shippensburg homes and rentals, the best outcome is a lock that turns smoothly, closes cleanly, and fits the way the property is actually used — whether that means a long-lived front door on a historic street or a busy unit near campus that needs sturdier everyday hardware.

When should a lock be replaced instead of rekeyed in Shippensburg?

Replacement makes more sense when the lock body is worn out, the keyway is damaged, the latch sticks, or the hardware no longer fits the door properly. Rekeying changes who can use the lock; replacement fixes the hardware itself. On older King Street homes, wear and door movement often push the job toward replacement.

What kinds of residential locks do you install?

We install common residential brands like Kwikset, Schlage, Baldwin, Yale, Medeco, and Mul-T-Lock, depending on the door and security needs. That can include knob locks, deadbolts, and keyed-alike sets. We also look at ANSI Grade 1, 2, or 3 hardware so the replacement matches the door's use.

What makes lock replacement harder on older Shippensburg homes?

Historic homes near King Street often have shifted frames, painted-over hardware, worn screw holes, or mismatched door prep from past repairs. A lock may need more than a simple swap if the bore, backset, or strike plate doesn't line up. Careful measurement and adjustment are part of doing it right.

Can you replace locks at student rentals near Shippensburg University?

Yes. Rental turnover can expose worn locks, loose hardware, or outdated keying. We can replace damaged exterior locks, upgrade deadbolts, and set up keyed-alike entry points when that fits the property. The main thing to have ready is access to the doors and any information about which entries should work with the same key.

What should I know before calling for a lock replacement?

It helps to know whether the door is wood, metal, or another material, and whether the problem is on a front door, side door, or interior entry. If you still have a working key, bring it. The brand name on the lock, the door thickness, and whether the door has a deadbolt all help us bring the right hardware.
