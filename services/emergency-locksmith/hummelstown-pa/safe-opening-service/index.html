Good to know

Common questions.

Can you open a safe without drilling it?

Often, yes. Many residential and office safes can be opened by dialing, manipulating the lock, decoding a combination, or using approved override methods, depending on the safe and lock type. Drilling is usually a last resort when the mechanism is damaged, the lock has failed, or the contents are at risk and a non-destructive method is not workable.

What information helps you open my safe faster?

The make, model, serial number if visible, lock type, and anything that happened right before the problem started are all useful. If you have the manual or original key for a bypass lock, keep it handy. Knowing whether it is mechanical, electronic, or dual-lock helps narrow the opening method and reduces guesswork on site.

Do you service both home safes and office safes in Hummelstown?

Yes. We open and service many residential and office safes in Hummelstown, including homes near the historic square, properties along Main Street, and businesses close to Swatara Creek or Route 322. Different safes use different locking systems, so the opening method depends on the model, the lock grade, and the condition of the door and boltwork.

What makes a safe harder to open?

High-security locks, relocking devices, damaged keypads, worn dials, stuck boltwork, and prior forced-entry damage can all make a job more complex. Fire safes with thick insulation or safes that were left open and then slammed shut can also create problems. The more the lock or door has been compromised, the more careful the opening process has to be.

What should I do if my safe stops opening in the evening in Hummelstown?

Do not keep forcing the handle or spinning the dial hard, because that can worsen the failure. Gather the safe's brand and model, note any error messages or unusual resistance, and keep the area clear so the lock can be inspected.
