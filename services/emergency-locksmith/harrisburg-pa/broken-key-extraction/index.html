What broken key extraction actually involves

A snapped key does not automatically mean the lock is done for. The first question is whether the cylinder can be saved or whether the damage calls for replacement. For broken key extraction, we start by looking at how much of the blade is still visible, what kind of lock is involved, and whether the key broke because of wear, a bent bow, frozen pins, or a damaged plug. On many Kwikset, Schlage, Yale, or Baldwin locks, a clean break with part of the key sticking out can often be handled by careful extraction, then the lock can be checked, repinned, and tested before a new key is cut.

The actual process is methodical. We use the right extractor hooks, picks, pinning tools, and lubrication to avoid chewing up the keyway. If the fragment is seated deep, the plug may need to be rotated just enough to relieve pressure on the pins. After the piece comes out, we inspect the wafers or pin stacks, the keyway, and the edge cuts on the new key. Some locks are worth repairing because the hardware is sound; others need replacement if the cylinder is scarred, the plug is cracked, or the key snapped from internal wear. A smarter call up front saves you from a second failure later.

Good to know

Common questions.

Can a broken key be extracted without replacing the lock?

Often, yes. If part of the blade is still reachable and the cylinder is not damaged, extraction can be done without replacing the hardware. After the fragment comes out, the lock is tested. If the plug, pins, or keyway are scarred, replacement may be the better call.

What makes broken key extraction harder?

A fragment that is buried deep in the keyway, a bent or jammed plug, rust, dirt, or a lock that was already wearing out can make the job tougher. High-security cylinders like Medeco or Mul-T-Lock also require more care because the keyways and pinning are more exacting.

Can you make a new key after extracting the broken one?

Yes. If the original key can be decoded or the lock can be read, a new key can be cut and tested after extraction. For vehicles, that may involve a transponder or smart key process with a programmer. For homes, the key can often be duplicated or originated from the lock.

Do you work on door locks, deadbolts, and car keys?

We handle all three when the key breaks. Home deadbolts, knob locks, and commercial cylinders can often be repaired or rekeyed. Vehicle keys may need a different process, especially if the key is laser-cut, sidewinder, or includes a chip that has to be programmed.
