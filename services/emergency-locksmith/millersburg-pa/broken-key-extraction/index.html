What broken key extraction actually involves

What most people get wrong about a broken key is thinking the lock is the problem. Often, the lock is still usable; the key snapped because of wear, a bent blade, cold metal, or a cylinder that was already sticky. The first job is to look at how much of the key is left in the plug, whether the fragment sits flush or broke deep inside, and whether the lock is a simple residential cylinder, a Schlage or Kwikset knob, a deadbolt, or a car door or ignition. From there, extraction is usually a careful non-destructive process with picks, extractors, and light decoding work rather than forcing the plug and making the damage worse.

In Millersburg, that might mean helping someone at the town square, along Market Street, or out near Route 147 and Route 209 where a stuck fragment can stop the whole day. After the broken piece is removed, we check the keyway and pin stack for burrs or bent pins, then cut or originate a working key from the code, a readable key, or vehicle data if it is a transponder or smart key situation. Some jobs are straightforward; others are harder because the key sheared off while turning, the lock is corroded from weather, or the cylinder was already worn out. Good to know

Common questions.

What usually causes a key to snap in a lock?

Wear is the usual culprit. Keys bend a little over time, especially if the lock is sticky, the cylinder is dirty, or someone turns the key under tension. Cold weather, rust, and forcing an old key into a worn Schlage, Kwikset, or car ignition can all make the blade break at the shoulder.

Can you remove a broken key without replacing the whole lock?

Often, yes. If the fragment is accessible and the cylinder is not damaged, extraction can be done non-destructively with the right picks and extractors. After that, the lock is checked for bent pins, burrs, or misalignment. A replacement is only needed if the cylinder is too worn or damaged to work properly.

What should I do before calling for broken key extraction?

Leave the keyway alone and do not push the broken piece deeper. If you have the key head, any spare key, or the vehicle information handy, that helps. For cars, the make, model, year, and whether it uses a transponder or proximity key can save time once the fragment is out.

Can a broken car key be replaced after extraction?

Yes. After the fragment is removed, we can usually cut or originate a new car key. For many vehicles, that means working from a code, VIN-based key origination, or a key programmer for transponder and smart keys. The exact process depends on the vehicle make and the system it uses.
