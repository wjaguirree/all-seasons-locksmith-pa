What broken key extraction actually involves

The problem usually announces itself with a twist that feels wrong: the key turns partway, the bow stays in your hand, and the broken blade is still buried in the cylinder. In Lebanon, that happens in older house locks around Downtown Lebanon, in deadbolts near the Cornwall Road area, and in vehicle ignitions after a rough start along Route 422 or Route 72. Broken key extraction is not just pulling metal out. A locksmith first checks whether the lock body, plug, or keyway is damaged, then decides between non-destructive picking, careful probing, or using extraction tools that grab the fragment without chewing up the pins. If the key snapped because the lock is worn, the fix may include rekeying or replacing parts, especially on Kwikset, Schlage, Yale, Baldwin, or higher-security cylinders.

What makes the job easier is a key stub that sits proud of the face of the lock, a clean break, and a cylinder that still rotates normally. It gets harder when the fragment is flush, when the keyway is packed with debris, or when a high-security system like Medeco or Mul-T-Lock needs special handling. For vehicles, the process can differ again: transponder keys, laser-cut sidewinder keys, and smart/proximity systems may require decoding, VIN-based key origination, or key programmer work after the broken piece is removed. If you can, have the door open if it is not a lockout, know the vehicle make and model, and tell the technician whether the key broke in the house lock, deadbolt, ignition, or padlock. That helps us choose the right method before we arrive.

Good to know

Common questions.

What should I do right after a key breaks off in the lock in Lebanon?

Stop turning it. If the broken piece is still visible, leave it alone and call for extraction before the fragment gets pushed deeper. Don't use glue, pliers, or a screwdriver. Those usually make the cylinder harder to save, especially on older home locks common in Lebanon and on vehicle ignitions.

Can a locksmith get the broken key out without replacing the whole lock?

Often, yes. If the cylinder is intact and the fragment hasn't damaged the plug or pins, a locksmith can extract the piece and make a working key again. If the lock was already worn, we may recommend rekeying or replacing the cylinder so you are not back in the same situation.

How do you remove a broken key from a car ignition?

The locksmith checks whether the blade is flush, how the wafers are sitting, and whether the ignition still turns. Then we use extraction tools, decoding methods, and careful manipulation to remove the fragment without scratching the cylinder. On some transponder or smart-key vehicles, a new key may also need programming.

Do you handle high-security locks like Medeco or Mul-T-Lock?

Yes, but those systems need a more careful approach than standard residential hardware. Their keyways and pinning are tighter, so extraction has to protect the cylinder instead of forcing the fragment out. In some cases, the better fix is to replace or rekey the cylinder after the broken piece is removed.
