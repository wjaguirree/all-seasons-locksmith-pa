Good to know

Common questions.

How do you open a house door without damaging the lock?

The first step is identifying the lock type and the door condition. When the hardware allows it, I use non-destructive picking or decoding instead of forcing the knob or deadbolt. If the door is swollen, painted shut, or the lock is worn out, I may need a different method, but the goal is always to protect the door and hardware first.

What should I have ready before you come for a house lockout in Lebanon?

Have a way to prove you live there, such as an ID with the address or another document tied to the home. If you can, tell me whether the door has a knob lock, deadbolt, or smart lock, and whether the key is lost, broken, or locked inside. That helps me choose the right tools when I arrive.

Can you open older locks on homes in Downtown Lebanon or North Lebanon?

Yes, but older hardware can be more complicated. Paint buildup, worn pins, and loose strikes are common on older residential doors, especially around historic housing stock. I check the lock body, cylinder, and alignment before I start, because an older Schlage, Kwikset, or Baldwin lock may need a different approach than a newer one.

What if my lockout is caused by a broken key or a high-security lock?

A broken key can block the keyway, which changes the entry method right away. High-security cylinders such as Medeco or Mul-T-Lock are built to resist standard picking, so I may need specialized decoding or controlled entry techniques. If the lock is damaged internally, I'll tell you whether repair or rekeying makes more sense after the door is open.
