Good to know

Common questions.

Can you open my house door without breaking the lock?

In many cases, yes. The first goal is non-destructive entry using picking or decoding methods matched to the lock. That works best on standard residential cylinders that are not badly damaged. If the keyway is broken, the lock is jammed, or the door is badly misaligned, the method changes, but the goal stays the same: open the door with the least possible damage.

What should I have ready when I call for a house lockout in Shippensburg?

Have your exact location ready, including whether you're near Shippensburg University, downtown King Street, or off Route 11 or I-81. Be ready to show that you live there or have permission to enter. It also helps to describe the lock: deadbolt, knob lock, keypad, or smart lock. That tells the locksmith what tools and parts may be needed.

What makes a house lockout harder to open?

A damaged key, a worn-out cylinder, a warped door, or a deadbolt that no longer lines up with the strike can all make the job harder. High-security hardware and restricted keyways also take more specialized handling. Older homes in the historic district often have fit issues that are different from newer rental units near campus, so the approach has to match the door, not just the key.

If I'm locked out, should I just force the door?

No. Forcing the door can bend the latch, crack the jamb, or leave the lock in worse shape than it started. That can turn a simple lockout into a door repair. If the lock itself is the problem, forcing it may also wipe out the chance of a clean opening. A careful non-destructive entry attempt is usually the better first move.

Do you rekey the lock after a lockout if I want old keys to stop working?

Yes, if that is the right fix for your situation. Rekeying changes the pins inside the cylinder so old keys no longer operate it, which is useful after a move, a key loss, or a rental turnover near Shippensburg University. If the hardware is worn or damaged, replacement may be the better choice than rekeying alone.
