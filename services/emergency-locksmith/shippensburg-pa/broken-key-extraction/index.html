What broken key extraction actually involves

Broken key extraction looks simple from the outside: the key snapped, the lock is stuck, and you need the rest of the blade removed without making the cylinder worse. In practice, the first question is whether the break is clean and exposed or buried deep in a pin tumbler lock. A straightforward job is usually a standard residential doorknob or deadbolt on a Kwikset, Schlage, Yale, or Baldwin lock, where the fragment can be reached with extraction picks, tweezers, or a broken-key tool. It gets more complicated when the key breaks flush in a worn cylinder, the lock is high-security like Medeco or Mul-T-Lock, or the hardware is damaged from forcing the key before it snapped.

The process starts with examining the lock body, keyway, and what remains of the key. If the cylinder is still aligned, a locksmith may use non-destructive picking, lubrication, and a decoding approach to pull the fragment out intact. If the key blade is bent, sheared, or packed behind the plug, the tech may have to remove the cylinder, extract the piece on the bench, and then test whether the lock still turns smoothly. When the key is out, the next step is making sure you still have a working key or can create one by code, key blank, or VIN-based key origination for vehicles. What makes the job easier is a lock that hasn't been jammed repeatedly; what makes it harder is broken internal springs, debris, or a key that snapped because the cylinder itself is failing.

Good to know

Common questions.

How do you remove a broken key without damaging the lock?

The first step is to inspect how the key broke and how far the fragment is set inside the keyway. If enough metal is exposed, a locksmith can often use extraction tools and controlled picking to lift it out without disassembling the lock. If it sits too deep or the cylinder is worn, removing the cylinder is sometimes the safer move.

Can you make a new key after the broken piece is removed?

Yes, in many cases. Once the fragment is out, the locksmith can determine whether the lock can still be keyed from the remaining cut pattern, from a key code, or by reading the lock itself. For vehicles, the next step may be transponder programming, laser-cut cutting, or VIN-based key origination depending on the make and model.

What makes broken key extraction harder in older Shippensburg homes?

Older cylinders around historic King Street can be more worn, which means the key may have snapped because the lock itself is tight or misaligned. When the plug, springs, or pins are damaged, extraction alone may not solve the problem. The lock may need to be cleaned, rekeyed, or replaced after the fragment is removed.

Can you extract a broken car key from the ignition?

Usually, yes, but the approach depends on the vehicle. A simple metal key is handled differently than a transponder, smart/proximity, or sidewinder key. The technician will check whether the fragment is in the door, trunk, or ignition, then decide whether non-destructive extraction, decoding, or a programmed replacement key is the best route.

What should I have ready when I call for broken key extraction in Shippensburg?

Have the lock location, the vehicle make and model if it's a car key, and a clear description of where the key snapped. If you're near Shippensburg University, downtown King Street, or off Route 11 or I-81, mention that so the locksmith can plan the quickest approach. If you still have part of the key, keep it.
