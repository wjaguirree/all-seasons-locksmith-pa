What broken key extraction actually involves

Most people think a broken key job is just pulling metal out of a lock. That is the part you see; the real work is figuring out why the key snapped and whether the cylinder survived the break. On a call in Hershey, that can mean a house lock near Downtown Hershey, a rental door in Deer Run, or a vehicle lock along Route 39 where the key twisted before it broke. We start by checking the lock and the broken fragment, then decide whether non-destructive picking, careful extraction tools, or a full disassembly makes the most sense. If the key is still protruding, it may come out cleanly. If it broke flush, the cylinder may need to be decoded, pinned, or rebuilt before a new key will work smoothly.

What changes the process is the lock brand and the condition of the hardware. A worn Schlage or Kwikset cylinder may release the fragment differently than a Baldwin, Yale, Medeco, or Mul-T-Lock lock. High-security keys and tight tolerances leave less room for error, and a bent keyway or damaged pins can turn a simple extraction into a repair. For vehicles, we may also deal with transponder, laser-cut, sidewinder, or smart keys on Toyota, Honda, Ford, Chevrolet, Nissan, Hyundai, Kia, Jeep, RAM, Subaru, or BMW models. Once the broken piece is out, we check the key cut or key code, then make sure the replacement works before we hand it back.

The best thing you can do is leave the lock alone. More force usually drives the fragment deeper and can scar the plug. If you still have the broken half, bring it with you. If the issue is at Hersheypark, the Hershey Medical Center, or a driveway off Hersheypark Drive, tell us whether it is a door lock, deadbolt, ignition, or padlock so we can bring the right extraction tools and key machine setup the first time.

Good to know

Common questions.

What is the first thing a locksmith checks before extracting a broken key?

First, we look at how the key failed and where the fragment sits in the keyway. A piece sticking out is easier to remove than one broken flush. We also check the lock type, because a standard Schlage or Kwikset cylinder, a high-security Medeco lock, and a vehicle ignition all need different extraction methods.

Can a broken key be removed without replacing the whole lock?

Often, yes. If the pins, plug, and keyway are still in good shape, the fragment can be extracted and the lock used again. If the break damaged the cylinder, bent the keyway, or left the lock binding, the next step may be a rekey or a cylinder replacement rather than forcing the original hardware.

What kinds of keys break most often in locks and ignitions?

Worn brass house keys, older automotive keys, and tight-fitting high-security keys can all snap if the lock is dirty or the cylinder is worn. In vehicles, transponder, laser-cut, sidewinder, and smart keys can fail differently depending on the model, but the common problem is still stress on a key that was already weakened.

Will I need a new key after the broken piece comes out?

Usually, yes, because the old key failed for a reason. We can often cut a new key from the remaining half, the code, or the lock itself if needed. If it is a vehicle with a chip or proximity system, the new key may also need programming so the car recognizes it.
