What broken key extraction actually involves

A snapped key is more than an inconvenience. In Steelton, it can leave a front door, shop door, or deadbolt sitting half-secured while traffic keeps moving along Front Street and shifts change around the steelworks. The first step is to stop forcing the lock. Pushing the broken blade deeper can damage the pins, bend a plug, or turn a simple extraction into a full lock repair. We inspect the keyway, identify the lock brand and cylinder type, and check whether the break is visible or buried past the shear line.

From there, the work depends on the hardware. A standard Kwikset or Schlage cylinder may respond to careful non-destructive picking and extraction tools, while higher-security Medeco or Mul-T-Lock hardware can take more decoding and patience. If the break is clean and the lock is still aligned, we use extraction hooks, pinning tools, and lubricant to pull the fragment without chewing up the keyway. If the key is part of a transponder, laser-cut/sidewinder, or smart/proximity setup, we can often originate a working replacement after the lock is cleared, using the remaining key pattern, VIN-based key origination where appropriate, or key programming when the vehicle or hardware calls for it. What makes it harder is a bent lock, worn pins, a jagged break, or a key that snapped off flush inside the cylinder.

Before we leave, we test the lock several times, check for hidden drag in the cylinder, and make sure the new key turns smoothly. For the fastest result, have the property address, the lock brand if you know it, and any matching key or vehicle paperwork ready. In the borough rowhomes and along Route 230, that kind of quick information helps us move straight into the extraction and get the lock back in service.

Good to know

Common questions.

Should I try to pull a broken key out of the lock myself?

Usually not. If the blade is still seated in the pin stack, tweezers or improvised tools can push it deeper and damage the cylinder. Stop using the keyway, keep the lock in its current position, and let a technician inspect it. A clean extraction is much easier when the fragment hasn't been forced past the shear line.

Can a broken key be removed without replacing the lock?

Often, yes. If the cylinder is intact and the break didn't bend the plug or scar the keyway badly, we can usually extract the fragment and test the lock afterward. Replacement only becomes necessary when the internal pins, springs, or plug are damaged enough that the lock no longer turns smoothly.

What happens during broken key extraction?

We first identify the lock brand and type, then inspect how the key snapped and how far the piece sits inside the keyway. After that, we use the right extraction hook, pick, or tension tool to bring the fragment out without damaging the cylinder. Once it's clear, we test the lock and confirm the new key works.

Can you make a working key after the broken piece is removed?

Yes, in many cases. If it's a home lock, we can cut a replacement from an existing key pattern or decode the cylinder. For vehicles, the process may involve VIN-based key origination, key cutting, and sometimes programming for transponder or smart/proximity systems. The exact method depends on the make, model, and lock type.

Is broken key extraction harder on certain locks or in Steelton rowhomes?

It can be. Older residential hardware, worn deadbolts, and tight door alignment in the borough rowhomes can make the cylinder bind and trap the fragment. High-security locks like Medeco or Mul-T-Lock also require more careful handling. Around Front Street and the mill area, we often see locks that have taken a lot of daily use and need closer inspection.
