What keyless entry systems actually involves

Keyless entry for a business is not just a keypad on the wall. It is a working system that has to fit the door, the latch, the frame, and the way people actually come and go on Derry Street. For a clean installation, the first step is checking the existing prep: bore size, backset, door swing, edge thickness, strike alignment, and whether the door is wood, aluminum, or steel. On commercial jobs, we also look at the lock grade and the hardware already in place, because an ANSI Grade 1 cylindrical lock, a mortise lock, or an electrified trim setup each calls for a different approach. The goal is always the same: keep the door usable while avoiding unnecessary drilling, damage, or replacement of parts that still work.

From there, the process depends on the system. A standalone keypad may need a new latch, reinforced strike, and proper code programming. A fob or card reader can involve credential enrollment, wiring checks, and a control unit that ties the door to scheduled access. For smart and access-control systems, we may use key programmers, pinning tools for backup cylinders, or VIN-based key origination when the lockset has keyed components that still need to match a master plan. What makes the job easier is a clear idea of who needs access and how many doors are involved. What slows it down is damaged hardware, misaligned frames, or old locks that were never standard to begin with. In a place like Paxtang, where businesses sit near tree-lined blocks and around Paxtang Park, we plan the work so the door closes right, latches cleanly, and stays secure without turning the installation into a teardown.

If a storefront or office along Derry Street wants to move away from metal keys, the actual job usually starts with the door itself, not the keypad. We test the latch throw, confirm whether the existing deadlatch or mortise body can stay, and decide whether the system should be standalone, networked, or tied into an access-control panel. That approach matters because many failures come from forcing a new reader onto the wrong hardware. A good install uses the least invasive method that still gives proper security and reliable entry.

Good to know

Common questions.

How does a commercial keyless entry installation avoid damaging the door?

A careful install starts with measuring the existing prep and testing the current lock before anything is cut or drilled. The technician checks bore size, backset, edge thickness, strike alignment, and door material. If the hardware can be reused, it should be. If not, the replacement should match the door instead of forcing the door to match the hardware.

What kinds of keyless entry systems work for businesses in Paxtang?

For small offices and storefronts near Derry Street, common options include keypad locks, fob readers, card access, and full access-control systems. The right choice depends on how many users need entry, whether the door is interior or exterior, and whether you need simple code changes or scheduled access with audit capability.

Can a keyless system be added to an existing commercial lock?

Often, yes. Some doors can keep the existing lock body while adding a keypad, electrified trim, or access-control hardware. Other doors need a new cylindrical, mortise, or cylindrical deadlatch setup. The deciding factors are door prep, lock grade, frame condition, and whether the latch and strike line up correctly.

What should I have ready before a locksmith works on a business door?

Have the door location, the type of current lock, and any details about who needs access. If there is an existing keypad, fob system, or control panel, that information helps. It also helps to know whether the door is wood, steel, or aluminum, and whether the problem is installation, repair, or a full upgrade.

Do keyless entry systems still use a backup key?

Many commercial systems do. Some keypad and smart-lock setups keep a mechanical cylinder for backup entry, and that cylinder may need to be keyed to a master plan or matched to other doors. If the backup key is part of the system, the locksmith can often originate or rekey it so the keyless and keyed access work together.
