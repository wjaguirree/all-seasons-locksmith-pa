What commercial lock rekeying actually involves

Commercial lock rekeying sounds simple, but the job changes a lot depending on what you're working with. On older properties along Derry Street, a locksmith may find worn Schlage, Kwikset, or Baldwin cylinders that still operate well enough to keep, but need new key control after a manager leaves or keys go missing. On newer storefronts or offices, the hardware may be ANSI Grade 1 or Grade 2, with tighter tolerances, restricted keyways, or even smart-cylinder components that need to be checked before anything gets repinned. The goal is the same: keep the lock body in place, change the key that operates it, and restore control without replacing every piece of hardware.

The process starts with identifying each lock, checking whether it's a standard pin tumbler cylinder, a mortise lock, a rim cylinder, or a deadbolt on a service door. From there, the tech removes the cylinder, reads the keyway, and repins it with the correct kit so the old key no longer works. If a business uses high-security hardware such as Medeco or Mul-T-Lock, or a restricted Yale or Schlage keyway, the procedure may require specific blanks and careful decoding rather than a quick swap. Rekeying is usually easier when the hardware is intact and the keys are accounted for; it gets harder when pins are worn, plugs are damaged, or multiple doors were keyed differently over time.

For Paxtang businesses, especially the smaller operations near Paxtang Park and along the shaded blocks, the practical advantage is control: one updated key plan instead of a full hardware replacement. A good rekey also gives the locksmith a chance to spot loose latch hardware, misaligned strikes, or cylinders that should be upgraded before they fail.

Good to know

Common questions.

What is the difference between rekeying and replacing a commercial lock?

Rekeying changes the pins inside the cylinder so old keys no longer work, while the lock body stays on the door. Replacement means installing a new lockset or cylinder assembly. In Paxtang, rekeying is often the better move when the hardware is still solid and you just need to reset access after staff turnover.

Can you rekey older commercial locks on Derry Street?

Usually, yes, as long as the cylinders and plug are still in good shape. Older hardware may use common cylinders from Schlage, Kwikset, or Baldwin, but wear, corroded pins, or mismatched parts can slow things down. The tech checks each lock first and decides whether a clean rekey is possible.

What kinds of commercial locks can be rekeyed?

Most standard pin tumbler commercial locks can be rekeyed, including many knob locks, lever locks, deadbolts, mortise cylinders, and some rim cylinders. High-security systems like Medeco or Mul-T-Lock can also be serviced, but they often need the correct key blanks and a more precise process. Smart or proximity hardware may involve both mechanical and electronic steps.

What should I have ready before a locksmith rekeys my business?

Have the doors accessible, any existing working keys gathered up, and a clear list of which locks should share the same key. If possible, note any doors that stick, latch hard, or have keys that already feel rough. That helps the locksmith sort out whether the issue is just key control or a hardware problem too.

Do newer commercial locks on newer buildings need a different rekey process?

They can. Newer hardware may have tighter tolerances, restricted keyways, or security pins that need exact matching during repinning. Some systems also use smart or proximity features that do not behave like a plain mechanical cylinder. The locksmith has to identify the brand and keyway first so the rekey matches the hardware instead of fighting it.
