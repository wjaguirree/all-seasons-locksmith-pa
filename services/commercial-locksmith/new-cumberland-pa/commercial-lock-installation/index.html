What commercial lock installation actually involves

When we arrive for a commercial lock installation in New Cumberland, the first thing we check is the door itself, not the lock box. A storefront or office door on Bridge Street can have wear at the hinges, a sagging frame, old bore holes from a past lever set, or a strike plate that never lined up correctly. If the door is out of square, even a solid commercial lock will bind, drag, or fail to latch. We measure the opening, inspect the metal or wood prep, check the handing, and confirm whether the hardware needs to meet an ANSI Grade 1, Grade 2, or Grade 3 spec for the job.

From there, the installation is about matching the lock to the use of the space. A small office may need a Schlage or Kwikset commercial lever set with a keyed entry function, while a storefront near downtown Bridge Street may call for heavier hardware, a mortise lock, or a high-security cylinder from Medeco or Mul-T-Lock. We may pin cylinders, decode an existing keyway, or originate keys by code or VIN when the lock setup ties into other doors or access points. The process can be simpler on a newer door and more involved when we have to repair damaged prep, adjust closers, or re-drill for proper latch engagement. We set the hardware, test the throw, verify the key action, and make sure the door closes and locks cleanly before we leave.

In a place like New Cumberland, where the riverfront blocks, the walkable downtown, and traffic off Route 15 all feed into everyday business traffic, the details matter. A lock that looks right but doesn't latch right is a problem waiting to happen.

Good to know

Common questions.

What do you check first before installing a commercial lock on a store or office door?

We start with the door, frame, and existing prep. If the door is sagging, the strike is off, or the bore holes are wrong, the best lock in the world will still operate poorly. We also check handing, thickness, and whether the hardware needs to be a mortise, cylindrical, or lever-style commercial set.

Can you install commercial locks on Bridge Street storefronts with glass or metal doors?

Yes. Storefront doors often need hardware matched to the door material and the way the opening is built. Glass storefronts, aluminum frames, and hollow metal doors each need different prep and mounting methods. We verify the latch alignment, reinforcement, and cylinder fit before installing anything so the lock seats properly and closes cleanly.

What types of commercial locks are commonly used for offices and shops?

Common choices include commercial lever sets, deadbolts, mortise locks, and high-security cylinders. For some doors, Schlage, Kwikset commercial, Baldwin, Yale, Medeco, or Mul-T-Lock components may be appropriate depending on the grade and security goal. The right choice depends on door type, traffic level, and whether you need keyed entry, storeroom, or classroom function.

How does a locksmith decide if a lock should be Grade 1, Grade 2, or Grade 3?

That depends on how much use the door gets and how much abuse the hardware can handle. Grade 1 is the heaviest duty, often chosen for high-traffic or higher-security applications. Grade 2 is common for many commercial doors, while Grade 3 is lighter duty. We match the grade to the opening instead of forcing one style everywhere.

What should I have ready when you come to install locks for a business in New Cumberland?

It helps to know which doors are getting new hardware, whether existing keys should still work, and if the lock needs to match other doors. If possible, have the current key, the old lock or cylinder, and a clear idea of who needs access. That saves time deciding on the spot and helps us set up the door correctly the first time.
