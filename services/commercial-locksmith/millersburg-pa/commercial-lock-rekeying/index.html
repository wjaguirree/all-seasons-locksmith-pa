What commercial lock rekeying actually involves

Commercial lock rekeying is the cleanest way to control who can open a business after staff changes, lost keys, or a change in occupancy. The lock body stays in place. What changes is the pinning inside the cylinder, so the old key no longer works and a new key does. For shops along Market Street in Millersburg, that matters when you want access reset without tearing up good hardware or replacing every knob, lever, or deadbolt on the door.

The work starts by identifying the lock brand and cylinder type, whether it's a Kwikset, Schlage, Baldwin, Yale, or a higher-security setup like Medeco or Mul-T-Lock. Then the tech removes the cylinder or tailpiece assembly, checks for wear, and pins it to a new key using the correct pinning kit and key blanks. On commercial doors, we also look at ANSI Grade 1, 2, or 3 hardware, because a worn latch, weak strike, or damaged cylinder can make rekeying less reliable. If a lock has been poorly maintained, already been altered, or uses restricted keyways, the process can take more care. Good rekeying should leave the door operating the same way it did before, just with a new key lineup.

Because this is a fully mobile service, we handle the work on site around the town square, near the riverside, or off Route 147 and Route 209 without taking the door out of service longer than needed. For many Millersburg businesses, that means the lock stays in place, the door keeps its existing fit, and the security reset happens with minimal disruption.

Good to know

Common questions.

What does commercial lock rekeying actually change?

Rekeying changes the pin stack inside the cylinder so the old key no longer lifts the pins to the correct heights. The outside hardware stays in place. It's the right move when you want to reset access after turnover, misplaced keys, or a tenant change without replacing every lock on the door.

Can you rekey storefront locks on Market Street without damaging the door?

Yes. A proper rekey is done by removing the cylinder or lock core, not by prying on the door or forcing the hardware. We inspect the lock, repin it to the new key, test operation, and reinstall it so the door keeps the same fit and finish.

What kinds of commercial locks can be rekeyed?

Most standard commercial cylinders can be rekeyed, including many Kwikset, Schlage, Baldwin, and Yale setups. Some higher-security systems like Medeco or Mul-T-Lock require specific parts and procedures. If the hardware is damaged, heavily worn, or uses a restricted keyway, we check the lock first before deciding the cleanest path.

What should I have ready before a rekey appointment in Millersburg?

Have the doors you want serviced accessible, and if possible, gather any existing keys you still have. It also helps to know which entrances need to match on one key and which should stay separate. For businesses near Route 147, Route 209, or the town square, clear access to the doors saves time on site.

Can all the locks in a small business be keyed alike?

Often yes, as long as the cylinders and keyways are compatible. That means one key can open multiple doors, which is useful for front, rear, and interior access. We check the lock models, cylinder condition, and security needs first, because some doors may be better kept on separate keys.
