What commercial lock installation actually involves

Commercial lock installation starts with the hardware, not the handle. A storefront on 2nd Street or an office near the State Capitol may need a Grade 1 deadbolt, a Schlage or Kwikset keyed lever, a Yale or Baldwin trim set, or a high-security cylinder from Medeco or Mul-T-Lock. The first step is checking the door, frame, hinge side, and existing prep: a lock can fail because the strike is loose, the latch doesn't fully extend, the door sags, or the cylinder no longer turns cleanly. On older doors, we may need to measure backset, crossbore, and door thickness before anything is mounted.

From there, the process is careful and practical. We test the alignment, confirm whether the opening needs a mortise lock, cylindrical lock, panic hardware, or a surface-mounted deadbolt, then fit the new device so the bolt or latch lands cleanly in the strike. If the job calls for keyed-alike hardware, restricted keyways, or a master key setup, the pins are arranged with a pinning kit and the cores are verified before final assembly. For some doors, especially in busy Downtown and Midtown spaces, non-destructive picking or decoding lets us keep the door usable while we work. What makes the job easier is having the door open, the existing lock accessible, and any key control needs already decided; what makes it harder is warped wood, damaged metal, or mismatched old prep.

Good to know

Common questions.

What kind of commercial lock is best for a storefront in Harrisburg?

It depends on the door and the level of traffic. Many storefronts use heavy-duty cylindrical locks, mortise locks, or panic hardware paired with a deadbolt or high-security cylinder. For doors along 2nd and 3rd Street, the frame strength and latch alignment matter just as much as the brand name.

Can you install locks on older office doors near the State Capitol?

Yes, but older doors often need measurements before the lock can be chosen. We check backset, crossbore, door thickness, and strike position. If the existing prep is worn or off-center, the installation may need adjustment so the latch lands correctly and the door closes without binding.

Do you handle Medeco, Mul-T-Lock, Schlage, and Yale hardware?

Yes. Those brands are common in commercial work, especially when a business wants better key control or more durable cylinders. We can install, rekey, and in many cases key-alike multiple doors so staff have a simpler system without giving up security.

What can make a commercial lock installation harder?

Warped doors, damaged frames, old screw holes, mismatched backsets, and previous hardware that was installed off-center all complicate the job. If the door is sagging or the strike plate is loose, we may need to correct the alignment first so the new lock doesn't fail the same way the old one did.

Can you help businesses near Midtown, Downtown, or the Broad Street Market?

Yes. We work across Harrisburg, including Midtown, Downtown, and the areas around the Broad Street Market, the Susquehanna riverfront, and the Capitol. For busy commercial openings, it helps if someone can point out which door is giving trouble and whether the goal is replacement, rekeying, or upgraded hardware.
