What commercial lock installation actually involves

Commercial lock installation usually starts with one question: does the existing hardware still make sense, or is replacement the better move? On storefronts and offices in Cornwall, that decision often comes down to wear, security needs, and how the door is built. A good technician checks the door, frame, strike, latch alignment, and existing prep before recommending a lockset. If the body is sound, rekeying or swapping parts may solve the problem. If the case is loose, the cylinder is worn, or the hardware is not the right grade for the opening, a new commercial-grade lock is the cleaner answer.

For most jobs, installation means measuring the backset and door thickness, confirming handing, and matching the hardware to the opening. That can mean a Grade 1 or Grade 2 cylindrical lock, a deadbolt, mortise hardware, or a lever set for an office suite. In some cases we install brands like Schlage, Kwikset, Baldwin, Yale, Medeco, or Mul-T-Lock, depending on the security level and the door prep. The process may include drilling or re-drilling, adjusting strike plates, pinning the cylinder, and testing the latch so the door closes without binding. Historic buildings around the Cornwall Iron Furnace and the historic blocks can add their own complications, especially when old frames have shifted or prior hardware was patched over.

That is why the first step is always inspection. A clean install should protect the door, work smoothly, and match the way the space is used day to day.

Common questions.

Should a commercial door in Cornwall be repaired or replaced?

If the lock still lines up, the cylinder is healthy, and the door/frame are in good shape, repair or rekeying may be enough. Replacement makes more sense when the hardware is loose, the latch is worn, the door is damaged, or the existing lock is the wrong grade for a storefront or office. An inspection settles it quickly.

What kind of commercial locks do you install on storefronts and offices?

That depends on the door and the security goal. Common options include cylindrical locksets, deadbolts, mortise locks, lever sets, and high-security cylinders. We work with commercial-grade hardware from brands like Schlage, Yale, Medeco, and Mul-T-Lock, and can match the setup to the way the door is used every day.

Can you install a new lock on an older building in Cornwall?

Yes, but older buildings often need more fitting work. Historic doors can have shifted frames, patched holes, or nonstandard prep from earlier hardware. We measure the door thickness, backset, and handing, then decide whether the new lock can fit existing openings or needs careful drilling and strike adjustment.

What should I have ready before a commercial lock installation?

Have the door accessible, know which entry is being worked on, and be ready to explain who needs keys or access. If you have the existing key, that can help with rekeying or matching cylinders. For offices or shops with multiple doors, it helps to identify which openings should be keyed alike and which should stay separate.
