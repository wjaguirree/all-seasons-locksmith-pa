What commercial lock rekeying actually involves

Commercial lock rekeying is the right move when keys have changed hands but the hardware still does its job. For shops and offices along historic King Street in Shippensburg, that usually means changing the key that works in the cylinder, not replacing every lock on the door. A good rekey starts with identifying the lock brand and cylinder type—common commercial hardware from Schlage, Kwikset, Yale, and Baldwin can often be serviced on site, while higher-security cylinders may call for specific pins, control keys, or restricted-key procedures. The goal is simple: keep the door, closer, deadbolt, and exit hardware intact, and make the old keys useless.

First the locksmith checks each lock for wear, broken set screws, misaligned parts, and whether the cylinder can be removed cleanly. Then the cylinder is taken apart, the old pins are removed, and new pins are matched to a new key by code, decoding, or key originating. On commercial jobs, the work can get more involved if there are master key systems, keyed-alike suites, or high-security cylinders that need special pinning kits. The technician tests the new key in the cylinder before reinstalling everything, then confirms the latch and deadbolt throw smoothly. That careful sequence matters in older downtown buildings, where shifting frames or tired hardware can make a quick rekey turn into a real adjustment job.

For offices near Shippensburg University or storefronts in the historic district, the practical benefit is control: when staff turnover, tenant changes, or misplaced keys become a problem, rekeying restores access without tearing out good hardware. If you can, have the building address, the number of doors, and any master-key or restricted-key information ready when you call.

Good to know

Common questions.

What does commercial lock rekeying actually change?

Rekeying changes the pins inside the cylinder so the old keys no longer work. The lock body, deadbolt, lever, and door hardware stay in place. For a Shippensburg business, that is often the cleanest way to regain control after staffing changes without replacing every lock on the property.

How do you rekey a commercial lock without damaging the door or hardware?

The technician removes the cylinder, disassembles it on site, swaps the pin stack to match a new key, then tests it before reinstalling. Done correctly, the door, trim, and latch stay untouched. If the frame is out of alignment or the lock is worn, those issues are checked before the cylinder goes back in.

Can all commercial locks in my King Street storefront be keyed to one key?

Often, yes, if the locks are compatible and in decent condition. The locksmith checks brand, cylinder type, and whether any door uses restricted or high-security hardware. If you have mixed brands or a master key system, some locks may need to be re-pinned differently to keep the right access levels in place.

What should I have ready before calling for a rekey in Shippensburg?

Have the building address, how many doors need service, and any information about existing master keys or restricted keys. It also helps to know whether the locks are on the storefront, office, rear entry, or interior doors. That lets the locksmith bring the right pinning kits and any brand-specific parts needed.

Is rekeying better than replacing locks after employee turnover?

In many cases, yes. If the hardware is still solid, rekeying is faster and keeps the existing locks in service. Replacement makes sense when the lock is damaged, outdated, or not secure enough for the building. On older downtown Shippensburg properties, a careful inspection decides which path is the better fit.
