What commercial lock installation actually involves

When a storefront lock starts sticking, a deadbolt feels sloppy, or an office door no longer closes the way it should, the first decision is not always replacement. A good commercial locksmith looks at the door, frame, hinges, strike, and lock body before recommending anything. In Shippensburg, that matters for shops and offices along historic King Street, where older openings can be out of square, and for newer commercial spaces near Route 11 or I-81, where heavier traffic can wear hardware faster than expected.

Commercial lock installation usually begins with identifying what is already on the door: knob lock, lever lock, mortise lock, cylindrical lock, or panic hardware. From there, the technician checks the door material, handing, backset, latch alignment, and whether the hardware is ANSI Grade 1, 2, or 3. If repair will hold, that may mean rekeying, tightening, replacing a worn cylinder, or correcting the strike. If replacement is the right call, the new lock is matched to the door and use case, then pinned, keyed, and fitted so the latch throws cleanly and the bolt seats without forcing the door. On some jobs, brands like Schlage, Kwikset, Baldwin, Yale, Medeco, or Mul-T-Lock may come into play depending on the building's security needs.

What makes the work harder is not just the lock itself, but the condition around it: warped wood, metal fatigue, commercial traffic, or a frame that has shifted over time. If you can, have the door accessible and know who needs keys or restricted access after the install. That helps the technician decide whether a simple repair, a rekey, or a full replacement is the cleanest path.

Good to know

Common questions.

Should my commercial door in Shippensburg be repaired or replaced?

That depends on the door, frame, and lock body, not just the symptom. If the latch is rubbing, the strike is misaligned, or the cylinder is worn, repair may solve it. If the lock case is damaged, the door is out of square, or access control needs have changed, replacement is usually the better choice.

What kinds of commercial locks can be installed on storefronts and offices?

Common options include cylindrical lever locks, mortise locks, deadbolts, and panic hardware for exit doors. Depending on the door and security level, we may install Schlage, Kwikset, Baldwin, Yale, Medeco, or Mul-T-Lock hardware. The right choice depends on the opening, traffic, and whether the door needs keyed access or high-security control.

How does a commercial lock installation usually work?

First the technician inspects the door, frame, handing, backset, and strike alignment. Then the existing hardware is tested to see whether repair is possible. If replacement is needed, the new lock is fitted, keyed or rekeyed as needed, and checked for smooth latch and bolt movement. The final step is making sure the door closes and locks without binding.

Can you install commercial locks on older buildings near historic King Street?

Yes, but older openings often need more careful fitting. Doors can be slightly out of square, and the strike or latch position may need adjustment before new hardware will work correctly. In some cases, the lock can be upgraded without changing the entire door, but the condition of the frame and wood or metal prep matters a lot.

What should I have ready before a commercial locksmith comes to my Shippensburg business?

It helps to know which door is giving trouble, who needs access after the job, and whether the lock is for an entrance, office, storage room, or exit door. If possible, keep the door accessible and be ready to describe whether the issue is sticking, loose hardware, broken keys, or a door that will not latch cleanly.
