What keyless entry systems actually involves

When a keypad, fob reader, or access-control door stops working, the first question is not what brand to buy next. It is whether the existing hardware can be repaired, reprogrammed, or should be replaced. On a shop door along Cumberland Street or a Route 422 business entrance, that decision starts with a close look at the lock body, the power source, the door prep, and how the system talks to the strike, mag lock, or controller. If the issue is a worn keypad, weak batteries, a failing reader, damaged wiring, or a lost admin code, repair may be the cleanest path. If the hardware is mismatched, obsolete, or not suited to the door and traffic level, replacement is usually the better answer.

The work itself is part locksmithing, part low-voltage troubleshooting. A tech may verify the latch or deadbolt alignment, check wiring continuity, test the controller, and confirm whether the system uses standalone PIN access, a fob database, or networked access control. For many commercial setups, brands like Kwikset, Schlage, Baldwin, Yale, Medeco, or Mul-T-Lock may appear on the door side, while the access side may involve keypad modules, card readers, or smart/proximity devices. Better hardware choices for busy entries often include ANSI Grade 1 or Grade 2 components paired with non-destructive fitting so the door still closes cleanly and the latch fully engages. The easiest jobs are the ones where the customer can show the current symptoms, confirm who has admin access, and identify whether the door is for staff, deliveries, or restricted entry.

Sometimes the best fix is not a new system at all, but a proper reset, new credentials, or a fresh set of keys for the mechanical override. Other times, especially on older doors in Downtown Lebanon or around older commercial buildings, the frame, trim, or strike location makes the whole setup unreliable until the hardware is rebuilt around the door.

Good to know

Common questions.

Should my keyless entry system be repaired or replaced?

If the problem is a weak battery, worn keypad, loose wiring, a bad strike, or a lost code, repair is often the first move. Replacement makes more sense when the hardware is outdated, the door prep is wrong, or the system keeps failing after programming and electrical checks. The right answer starts with the door, not the catalog.

What does a locksmith check on a commercial keypad or fob entry?

A commercial locksmith will usually inspect the door alignment, latch movement, power source, reader or keypad, wiring, and controller settings. If the setup includes a mechanical backup, that gets checked too. On some doors, the issue is not the electronics at all but a binding latch or a strike plate that is out of position.

Can keyless entry work with my existing business door hardware?

Often, yes, but not always cleanly. The door material, frame condition, closer, latch type, and existing bore prep all affect the result. A storefront in Lebanon may be a straightforward retrofit, while an older door might need reinforcement or new prep before a keypad, fob reader, or mag lock will behave correctly and close securely.

What kinds of commercial keyless entry systems are common?

Common setups include standalone PIN pads, fob readers, card access, and networked access-control systems. Some doors keep a keyed override, while others use smart/proximity credentials only. The best fit depends on how many people need access, whether the door is for staff or deliveries, and how tightly you want to control changes to entry permissions.
