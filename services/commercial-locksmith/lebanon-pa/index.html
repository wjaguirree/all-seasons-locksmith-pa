Commercial locksmith work in Lebanon

Commercial locksmith work starts with protecting the door and the hardware you already have. In Lebanon, that often means looking at older storefronts near Cumberland Street, offices along Route 422, or buildings in Downtown Lebanon where the lockset, closer, and frame may all be working against each other. A proper service call begins with checking the door alignment, hinge wear, latch condition, and whether the existing hardware is ANSI Grade 1, 2, or 3. If the lock is salvageable, the goal is repair or rekeying, not replacement. When a lock has to be opened without a working key, we use non-destructive methods first, then decode the cylinder or originate a replacement key with the right pins, blanks, and key programmer if the system calls for it.

From there, the job changes based on the hardware. A master key system has to be planned so tenant keys, manager keys, and restricted keys all function the way they should. Panic bar installation and door closer work require careful measurements so the door latches cleanly and still opens easily in an emergency. Keyless entry systems add another layer: credential programming, battery checks, strike or maglock compatibility, and making sure the bypass key still works. Some jobs are straightforward if the door is square and the hardware is standard Kwikset, Schlage, or Yale commercial-grade equipment. Others take more time when the opening is old, the lock is Medeco or Mul-T-Lock high-security, or the frame has been patched before.

Typical commercial calls around Lebanon

A shop on Cumberland Street may call because a side door sticks, the deadbolt drags, and employees are forcing the key every day. That is how cylinders get damaged and keys start snapping. The first step is to inspect the latch, strike, and closer, then figure out whether the issue is the lock body, the door sagging, or the frame not catching the bolt correctly. If the lock can be kept, rekeying and repair can restore control without changing the whole entry. If the hardware is worn beyond repair, a fresh commercial lock installation matched to the door is the cleaner fix.

Another common call comes from an office or clinic closer to the Lebanon VA Medical Center, where access control and door function have to be dependable all day. A manager may want a master key system for staff doors, or a panic bar installed on an exit that has to open fast but stay secure from the outside. In those jobs, the details matter: the cylinder depth, the latch throw, the closer speed, and whether the keyless entry system can handle the building's traffic. A tech should arrive ready to test the opening, verify the hardware type, and work from the door inward so the finish stays intact.

Good to know

Common questions.

How do you open a commercial door without damaging the lock?

The first move is to identify the lock type and the condition of the door, then use non-destructive methods like picking, bypass tools, or decoding when possible. If the cylinder is high-security or damaged, the technician may need to work the hardware apart carefully instead of forcing it. The goal is to preserve the door, trim, and frame.

What is involved in a commercial lock rekey?

Rekeying means changing the key pins inside the cylinder so old keys no longer work. The technician removes the core or disassembles the lock, matches the pin stack to a new key, and tests each turn for smooth operation. It is useful after staff changes, lost keys, or when you want one master system across multiple doors.

Can you set up a master key system for different doors in one building?

Yes, but it has to be planned around who needs access and when. The locksmith maps the doors, assigns change keys for individual areas, and builds a master key hierarchy so managers can open multiple locks while staff get limited access. The hardware must be compatible, and high-security cylinders may need special key control.

What should I know before installing a panic bar or door closer?

The door has to fit the hardware correctly, or the bar and closer will fight each other. A technician checks the door material, frame condition, latch position, and swing direction before drilling or mounting anything. If the closer is set too heavy or the latch is misaligned, the door may not close or latch cleanly.
