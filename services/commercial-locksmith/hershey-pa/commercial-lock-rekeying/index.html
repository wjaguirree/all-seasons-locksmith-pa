What commercial lock rekeying actually involves

It usually starts with a simple realization: an employee badge gets turned in, a key goes missing, or a manager can't account for who still has access to a back door on Chocolate Avenue. That is when commercial lock rekeying makes sense. Instead of replacing every lock, the cylinder is opened and repinned so the old key no longer works and a new key does. On commercial hardware from Kwikset, Schlage, Baldwin, Yale, Medeco, or Mul-T-Lock, the work begins by identifying the exact lock body, cylinder type, and keyway before anything is taken apart.

The process is mechanical, not guesswork. A technician removes the cylinder, reads the pin stack, and uses a pinning kit to set new driver and bottom pins to match the new key cut. On some doors, especially higher-security Medeco or Mul-T-Lock hardware, the method depends on whether the cylinder is standard, restricted, or part of a master-keyed system. If the door uses ANSI Grade 1 or Grade 2 hardware, the lock may also need a quick check for wear, misalignment, or stripped set screws so the rekey lasts. Job difficulty changes with worn keys, damaged cylinders, lost key control, or multiple doors that need to stay keyed alike. Having the existing key, any master-key records, and a clear list of which doors should match helps the work move faster.

Good to know

Common questions.

What does commercial lock rekeying actually change?

Rekeying changes the pins inside the cylinder so the old key stops working and a new key works instead. The lock body stays in place, which is why it's a common choice after staff turnover or when a key can't be accounted for. It's different from replacement; the access control is what changes, not necessarily the whole door hardware.

How do you rekey a commercial lock without replacing it?

The technician removes the cylinder, takes out the old pins, and installs a new pin combination to match a different key cut. On many commercial locks, that means using a pinning kit and checking the keyway for wear or damage. If the lock is part of a master-key system, the pinning has to preserve the access structure already in use.

Can every commercial lock in Hershey be rekeyed the same way?

No. Standard cylinders on Schlage, Kwikset, Yale, and similar hardware are usually straightforward, but restricted or high-security systems like Medeco and Mul-T-Lock may have special keyways or control rules. The technician has to identify the exact cylinder and sometimes confirm whether it is master-keyed before deciding how to repin it.

When is rekeying better than replacing the whole lock?

Rekeying is the better choice when the lock itself is in good shape and the concern is access, not failure. That is common after turnover, a lost key, or a change in who should enter stock rooms, offices, or rear entrances. If the cylinder is worn, damaged, or misaligned, replacement may make more sense.

Can you rekey commercial doors near Chocolate Avenue or the Hershey Medical Center without disrupting business?

Usually, yes. The work is done at the door, so the technician can assess the hardware, remove the cylinder, and set the new keying on site. What helps most is a clear list of which doors need to match, any existing master-key information, and the current keys in circulation. That keeps the job organized for shops, restaurants, and offices in Hershey.
