What keyless entry systems actually involves

Keyless entry for a business is only useful if the hardware is installed and serviced without chewing up the door, frame, or existing lock prep. On a shop or office along Route 422 in Annville, that usually starts with checking the door type, current bore size, latch alignment, and whether the opening can accept a keypad, fob reader, or fuller access-control setup without drilling extra holes. A clean install matters because many commercial doors in this part of Lebanon County already carry older hardware, and forcing the wrong body or strike into place can cause long-term problems.

The work itself is methodical. First we identify whether you need a standalone keypad, a networked access-control system, or an electrified lock tied to a door closer or strike. Then we verify power, wiring paths, and the lock grade on the door, since ANSI Grade 1 and Grade 2 hardware hold up better on busier entries than lighter residential-grade parts. If a key fob system is involved, we program the credentials and test each user profile. If the job uses a keypad on a storefront near downtown Annville or near Lebanon Valley College, we check entry flow, lock timing, and whether the latch throws cleanly every time. Some openings are simple; others need latch realignment, strike adjustment, or a different trim style so the installation stays non-destructive and serviceable later.

What changes the process most is the door itself. Older wood doors, metal storefronts, and mixed retrofits all behave differently, and a solid installer works around those limits instead of making the door fit the hardware. If you already know who needs access, how many users you want, and whether the system should keep a mechanical backup, that helps the setup go more smoothly.

Good to know

Common questions.

What's the difference between a keypad lock and a full access-control system for a business?

A keypad lock is usually a standalone unit on one door, with users entering a code at the opening. A full access-control system can use key fobs, cards, schedules, audit trails, and multiple doors. The right choice depends on how many people need entry, how often access changes, and whether you need the system tied to other hardware.

Can you install keyless entry without damaging the door?

That's the goal on most commercial jobs. We start by checking the existing bore, latch, and door material so the new hardware fits the opening cleanly. When possible, we reuse the current prep and make only the changes needed for the keypad, fob reader, or electrified lock. If the door is out of alignment, we correct that first.

What kinds of commercial doors work best with keyless entry systems?

Metal storefront doors, office doors, and many interior commercial openings can take keyless hardware well, but the door condition matters more than the label. We check thickness, latch type, hinge wear, and available wiring paths. Older doors near downtown Annville sometimes need strike adjustment or reinforcement before the new system will operate correctly.

Can a keyless system still keep a mechanical backup?

Yes, and on many business entries that is smart planning. A mechanical cylinder can stay in place as a backup, or the lockset can be paired with a keyed override. That helps if a credential is lost, a battery needs attention, or the access-control equipment ever needs service.
