What commercial lock rekeying actually involves

What most people get wrong about commercial lock rekeying is thinking the lock itself has to be replaced every time a key changes hands. In many shops and offices along Route 422 in Annville, that is not necessary. If the hardware is in good shape, rekeying lets us change which key operates the cylinder while keeping the door hardware in place. That is the right move after staff turnover, missing keys, or when an old key has been circulating too long.

The work starts with identifying the exact lock: Kwikset and Schlage are common on standard doors, while Baldwin, Yale, Medeco, or Mul-T-Lock may show up on higher-security entries or interior offices. We remove the cylinder, read the pinning pattern, and rebuild it with a new set of bottom and top pins so the old key no longer matches. On some commercial hardware, especially worn cylinders or mixed-key systems, we may recommend replacement instead of rekeying if the parts are damaged, pinned inconsistently, or already altered too many times.

For Annville businesses near Lebanon Valley College and downtown Main Street, the process is usually straightforward, but access control can complicate it. A front door, back door, file room, and supply closet may all need to be keyed differently or keyed alike on purpose. It helps to have the current key, any spare keys, and a clear list of which doors should open with which key. On site, we test each cylinder, check door alignment, and make sure the finished key turns cleanly without dragging.

Good to know

Common questions.

What is the difference between rekeying and replacing a commercial lock?

Rekeying changes the pins inside the cylinder so old keys no longer work, while the lock body and most hardware stay in place. Replacement is for worn, damaged, or outdated hardware. If the door closes well and the cylinder is sound, rekeying is often the cleaner way to regain key control.

What kinds of commercial locks can be rekeyed?

Most pin tumbler cylinders can be rekeyed, including common Schlage, Kwikset, Baldwin, and Yale hardware. Some higher-security systems like Medeco or Mul-T-Lock can also be serviced, but they may use specialized pins or key control parts. Very worn or modified cylinders may be better replaced than rekeyed.

What should I have ready before a commercial rekey service?

Have the current working key, any spare keys you want checked, and a list of doors that need to be changed. It also helps to know whether you want one master key for multiple doors or separate keys for different areas. Clear access to the hardware saves time once the technician arrives.

Can you rekey a shop or office near Lebanon Valley College or downtown Annville?

Yes. Businesses near Lebanon Valley College, downtown Main Street, and along Route 422 are common rekey jobs because staff changes and tenant turnover affect key control. We can rekey entries, offices, storage rooms, and back doors so old keys stop working without changing every lock on the building.

When is a commercial rekey harder than usual?

It gets harder when a lock has been re-pinned before, when the cylinder is worn, or when different doors use different brands and keyways. Misaligned doors can also make a lock feel like the problem when the real issue is the latch or strike. A careful inspection helps sort out what can be rekeyed cleanly.
