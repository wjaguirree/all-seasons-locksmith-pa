What commercial lock rekeying actually involves

When a key changes hands in an office, storefront, or shared workspace, the lock should change with it. If former staff, contractors, or copied keys are still out in circulation, the risk is not just inconvenience; it is unauthorized entry after hours, missing inventory, and no clear way to know who still has access. In Camp Hill, that matters for shops along Route 15, businesses on Trindle Road, and offices that sit between the Willow Park area and downtown Camp Hill. Commercial lock rekeying solves that problem without replacing every lock cylinder.

The process starts with identifying each lock by brand and keyway. Kwikset, Schlage, Yale, and Baldwin use different pinning layouts, and higher-security hardware such as Medeco or Mul-T-Lock can require different control methods. A locksmith removes the cylinder, reads or decodes the existing pin stack, and repins it so the old key no longer works. If the hardware is worn, misaligned, or missing parts, the job can get more involved. On-site work is usually best because a mobile locksmith can test every door, gate, and interior office lock before leaving.

For a business in Camp Hill, the details matter. A front door with an ANSI Grade 2 lever, a storage room deadbolt, and a file cabinet lock may all need different keys or a master key setup. We can also check whether your doors are working against you: loose hinges, damaged strikes, or keyed entry sets that no longer turn cleanly. Good to know

Common questions.

What does commercial lock rekeying actually change?

Rekeying changes the pin setup inside the cylinder so old keys stop working and new keys operate the same lock. The exterior hardware stays in place. For a business, that means you can tighten access after staff turnover, a lost key, or a tenant change without replacing every door lock.

Is rekeying different from changing the whole lock?

Yes. Changing the whole lock replaces the knob, lever, or deadbolt body. Rekeying keeps the existing hardware and only changes the internal pins and keys. If the lock is good quality and the body is sound, rekeying is usually the cleaner option for offices, shops, and storage areas.

Can you rekey master key systems in commercial buildings?

Often, yes. Master key systems use different pinning levels so one key opens multiple locks while subordinate keys open only certain doors. The locksmith has to map the existing hierarchy before changing anything. That matters in multi-door offices, retail spaces, and shared properties where access control has to stay organized.

What kinds of locks can be rekeyed on a commercial property?

Most standard pin-tumbler locks can be rekeyed, including many Schlage, Kwikset, Yale, and Baldwin cylinders. Some higher-security systems like Medeco or Mul-T-Lock may need specific parts or procedures. The condition of the hardware matters too; if a cylinder is badly worn or damaged, replacement may be the better choice.
