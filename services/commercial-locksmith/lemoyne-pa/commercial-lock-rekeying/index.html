What commercial lock rekeying actually involves

What most people get wrong about commercial lock rekeying is thinking it means changing the whole lock. It usually does not. For a business on Market Street in Lemoyne, rekeying means keeping the existing hardware and changing the internal pins so old keys stop working. That is often the cleaner move after an employee leaves, a key goes missing, or you want separate access for managers, cleaners, and tenants without replacing every knob, lever, or deadbolt.

The process starts with identifying each lock type on site, because a Schlage office lever, a Kwikset storefront deadbolt, and a Baldwin or Yale trim set do not all work the same way. A locksmith checks whether the cylinders are standard, restricted, or tied into a master key system, then removes the cylinder, reads the existing pinning, and repins it to a new key or key set. If the hardware is worn, misaligned, or damaged from heavy traffic near Route 15 and the Market Street corridor, rekeying may still work, but the lock may need repair or replacement to hold the new setup correctly.

Good commercial rekeying is as much about planning as hardware. A small office near Negley Park may only need one change, while a larger shop with multiple entry points may need keyed-alike access, different levels of access, or a master key plan that keeps front door, storage, and employee entrances organized. Having the current keys, any lock inventory you have, and a clear list of who should open what makes the job faster and less disruptive. The goal is simple: keep the building secure, keep the hardware you already own when it still makes sense, and make sure only the right keys work.

Good to know

Common questions.

What is the difference between rekeying a commercial lock and replacing it?

Rekeying changes the pins inside the cylinder so old keys no longer work, while the outside hardware stays in place. Replacement means installing a new lock body or cylinder assembly. Rekeying is usually the better first step when the lock works well but the keys have changed hands and you need access control tightened up.

Can you rekey several business doors to one key?

Yes, many commercial doors can be keyed alike so one key opens multiple locks. The locksmith first checks the brand, cylinder type, and condition of each lock, then repins them to match the same key. If the doors use different hardware families or the cylinders are too worn, keyed-alike setup may need extra parts or replacement cylinders.

How does a locksmith figure out whether my commercial locks can be rekeyed?

The tech identifies the lock brand and cylinder style, checks whether it is standard or part of a master key system, and inspects the plug and pins for wear. Common commercial hardware from brands like Schlage, Kwikset, Baldwin, or Yale is often rekeyable, but damaged or restricted cylinders can change the plan.

What should I have ready before a commercial rekey appointment?

Have the current keys, a list of doors that need to change, and any notes about who should keep access. If the building has a front entrance, back door, storage room, or separate tenant space, write that down. That helps the locksmith build the right key plan instead of making assumptions on site.
