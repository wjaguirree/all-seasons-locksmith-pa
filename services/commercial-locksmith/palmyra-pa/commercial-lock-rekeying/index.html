What commercial lock rekeying actually involves

Commercial lock rekeying is the cleanest way to cut off old key access after a manager leaves, an employee loses a key, or a tenant changes without tearing out good hardware. On shops and offices along Main Street in Palmyra, that usually means keeping the existing lock bodies, cylinders, and door prep in place while changing the key that opens them. A good rekey starts with identifying the brand and cylinder type, whether it is Kwikset, Schlage, Baldwin, Yale, or a higher-security setup like Medeco or Mul-T-Lock. From there, the locksmith removes the cylinder, reads the pin stack, and repins it to a new key using a pinning kit and the right key blanks.

The work should be non-destructive whenever possible. If the hardware is sound, there is no reason to drill, force, or replace every lock just because the key changed. What can complicate the job is mixed hardware on the same property, worn cylinders, broken tailpieces, master key systems, or doors that have been forced out of alignment. In those cases, the locksmith checks whether the lock can be decoded, rekeyed, or if the cylinder needs repair before the new key can be issued. For businesses near Route 422 or the Route 117 side of Palmyra, the goal is simple: secure access, preserve working hardware, and leave each door operating smoothly when the job is done.

Before the visit, it helps to know how many doors need to be changed, whether any locks are keyed alike, and whether a master key is in use. That lets the technician plan the pinning work and keep the rekey consistent across the property.

What does commercial lock rekeying actually change?

Rekeying changes the pins inside the cylinder so the old key no longer works and a new key does. The lock body, deadbolt, lever, or panic hardware usually stays in place. That makes it a practical option after staffing changes, lost keys, or when you want to tighten access without replacing every lock on the property.

Can you rekey commercial locks without damaging the door or hardware?

Yes, that is the normal goal. A locksmith removes the cylinder, works on the plug and pin stacks, and puts everything back together without altering the door prep or frame. If a lock is worn, bent, or previously repaired badly, the technician may need to service that hardware first so the rekey holds up properly.

What kinds of commercial locks can usually be rekeyed?

Most standard commercial cylinders from brands like Schlage, Kwikset, Baldwin, and Yale can be rekeyed if the parts are in good condition. Some high-security systems such as Medeco or Mul-T-Lock can also be serviced, but they may require special blanks, control procedures, or authorization. The exact method depends on the cylinder design.

How does a locksmith handle a master key system during a rekey?

Master key systems require more careful pinning because each cylinder may need to work on a user key and still respond to a master key. The locksmith has to match the stack depths correctly, keep records of the keying pattern, and test every door. A mixed or worn system can take longer because each cylinder must be checked individually.
