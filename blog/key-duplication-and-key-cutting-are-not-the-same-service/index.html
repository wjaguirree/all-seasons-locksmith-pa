Key Duplication vs Key Cutting in Harrisburg, PA

Ask a Harrisburg locksmith for a copy of your car key and you'll hear two words used like they mean one thing: duplication and cutting. For a plain brass house key, they nearly do. For a car key from the last couple of decades, they don't. Get only the first step done and the new key slides into the door, turns, and then the engine just sits there. Here's what each term covers, which keys need which step, and how to dodge the wrong job.

Quick answer: Key cutting shapes the metal blade so it matches the original. Key duplication is the whole job of producing a working copy. On a basic metal key those are the same thing. On a transponder car key, duplication means cutting the blade and then programming the chip so the car recognizes it.

What is key cutting?

Key cutting is the mechanical step of shaping a key blank so its ridges and grooves match a pattern. The blank sits in a vise on the machine, a guide traces the original key, and a blade cuts the blank to the same profile. According to Wikipedia's overview of key duplication, cutting is the primary method of duplicating a key. Metal only, nothing electronic.

So for plenty of everyday keys, cutting is the whole story. A padlock key. An older door key. A car key from before chips showed up. All of them only need the blade to be right.

What is key duplication?

Key duplication is the complete job of making a spare that works the way the original does. Cutting is only one piece. On many keys it's the only part. On a modern car key, you also have to get the vehicle to accept the new key.

Here's a quick test. Does the copy open the door and start the car? Then the duplication is done. Opens the door but won't start? Somebody finished the cutting and stopped there.

Why can a cut key still fail to start the car?

Since the late 1990s, most car keys have carried a small transponder chip, and the car's immobilizer reads it before the engine is allowed to run. A cutting machine can reproduce the blade. It can't hand the chip the right code for your vehicle. Skip that step and you're holding a key that looks right, fits the ignition, and still leaves the car dead.

How common are these chips? The Insurance Institute for Highway Safety reported in 2022 that even in model year 2015, immobilizers were standard on 96 percent of other manufacturers' vehicles, while Hyundai and Kia were the notable exception at 26 percent. So if you drive in Central PA, check your key rather than guess. A chip key and a plain one can look almost the same sitting on a keyring.

Why does that chip matter so much? A 2025 University of Leeds report on a Security Journal study by Professor Graham Farrell found that electronic engine immobilizers reduced vehicle theft in the United States by 80 percent over a 30-year period. The U.S. Department of Justice's CrimeSolutions program also rates engine immobilizers "Effective," based on one 2001 study that found a statistically significant drop in theft. Your spare has to satisfy that system, which is why the chip step can't be skipped.

Which key should you copy from?

Copy from the key in the best shape, ideally the original. A duplicate is only as accurate as whatever it was traced from, so a worn blade hands its wear to the copy. Ever owned a spare that sticks in the lock or needs a jiggle? That's usually a worn copy of a worn key. Wikipedia's key duplication overview flags the same weak spot: heavily worn keys are a known problem when a pattern grinder traces them.

If your only key is visibly worn, say so on the call. Sometimes a technician can cut from the vehicle's key code instead of tracing the worn blade, and the result is cleaner. Better to talk about it before the visit than after.

Does "Do Not Duplicate" stop a copy?

Short answer, no. Wikipedia notes that there is no direct legal sanction in the US for copying a key stamped "do not duplicate", unless it is a key you do not own. Think of the stamp as a polite request. Some shops honor it. Plenty don't.

Real protection comes from a restricted key system, where patented blanks mean only authorized dealers can make copies. Got a restricted key? Expect to be asked for proof of authorization. Only stamped? The stamp is a courtesy, not a lock.

Frequently asked questions

Is key cutting the same as key duplication?

Not always. Cutting shapes the metal blade, while duplication is the whole job of making a working copy. On a plain metal key the two match up. On a transponder key, duplication adds the chip programming.

Can a hardware store copy a transponder key?

Usually it can cut the blade. The chip is its own step and needs programming equipment for your vehicle, so ask whether they offer it. Don't assume the copy will start the car.

Can a locksmith copy a key marked Do Not Duplicate?

For a key you own, the stamp carries no direct legal force in the US. It's a request, not a barrier. Restricted keys with patented blanks work differently, since copies need authorization.

Do I need my original key to get a spare?

A working key makes duplication the easy route. Without one, you're looking at a replacement instead, and that can still happen on site. The car key replacement guide linked above covers it.
