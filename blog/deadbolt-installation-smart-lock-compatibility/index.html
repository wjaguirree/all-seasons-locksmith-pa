Deadbolt Installation and Smart Lock Compatibility for Central PA Homes

What does deadbolt installation involve, and will a smart lock work on my door?

Here's the short version. Deadbolt installation puts a steel bolt, a cylinder and a strike plate into your door. A smart lock is the same thing with a keypad or an app bolted on. Will one fit your door? Usually. But the door decides, not the box on the shelf.

Key takeaways

  • Check three things before you buy anything: the size of the holes in your door, the strength of the frame behind the strike plate, and how the smart lock connects to your phone or hub.
  • If all three line up, it's a one-visit job.
  • Read the back of the box, because a lock gets a separate rating for security, durability and finish.

A deadbolt is a lock with a solid bolt that you throw with a key, a thumb turn or a motor. A strike plate is the metal plate on the door frame that receives that bolt.

Plenty of people do this backwards. They buy the lock, get home, and find the hole is a quarter inch too small. Measure first.

What to check on the door first

A tape measure and five minutes. That's about it.

MeasurementCommon sizeWhy it matters
Door thickness1-3/8 to 1-3/4 inchesThin or thick doors need different bolt lengths or an adapter
Cross bore (large hole through the face)2-1/8 inchesA smaller hole means the lock body will not seat
Edge bore (small hole in the door edge)1 inchHolds the bolt, and decides whether the faceplate sits flush
Backset (edge to center of the large hole)2-3/8 or 2-3/4 inchesMost locks adjust, a few do not
Frame materialSolid wood, steel, or a thin jambA weak frame defeats a strong bolt

One term trips people up. The backset is the distance from the door edge to the center of the large hole, so measure it before you order.

Old houses in Harrisburg and Steelton are the wild card. Those doors have been planed and repainted for decades, and nothing is quite where the spec sheet says. A newer build in Mechanicsburg, for example, is usually stock. Measure anyway.

The Door-First Check

We run this routine before we put any lock on any door. We call it the Door-First Check. You can do it with a tape measure and a screwdriver.

  1. Measure the door. Thickness, cross bore, edge bore and backset, using the table above.
  2. Press on the frame. Lean on the jamb near the strike plate. If it gives, it needs reinforcing.
  3. Look at the strike plate screws. They should reach the stud behind the jamb. If they only grab the trim, a good kick opens the door.
  4. Close the door slowly. Does the bolt find its hole, or do you have to lift the door or shove it? A rubbing bolt wears out any lock.
  5. Decide what you want. Keypad? Remote unlock? A code for the dog walker? That answer picks the lock.

Step 4 fails more often than you'd think. Fix the door first. A deadbolt in a sagging door will jam on the first freezing morning, and you'll be the one standing outside.

Deadbolt ratings and what they tell you

Residential deadbolts are covered by ANSI/BHMA A156.40. The Builders Hardware Manufacturers Association announced a revision of that standard on April 28, 2025, and one of the listed changes is that it removed grade equivalencies (BHMA, 2025 standards announcement).

On the label, look for letters. BHMA's Secure Home program gives a C (Good), B (Better) or A (Best) rating in each of three categories, which are security, durability and finish (Secure Home, ratings combinations). A lock can earn an A for security and a B for finish, so turn the box over and read all three. Install quality and the door itself still change the result, and that's why the Door-First Check matters as much as the label does.

How smart lock compatibility works

Smart lock compatibility boils down to one question: how does the lock talk to your phone or hub? Four common answers, each with a catch.

ConnectionWhat it doesWatch for
BluetoothUnlocks from your phone when you are nearbyNo remote control unless you add a bridge
Wi-FiControls the lock from anywhereTends to use more battery, and depends on your router
Thread (with Matter)Low-power link that works with several smart home systemsNeeds a Thread border router, which many hubs and speakers include
Z-Wave or ZigbeeHub-based, common in older smart home setupsNeeds a matching hub in your home

Matter is the standard meant to get devices from different brands talking. The Connectivity Standards Alliance keeps a listing for every certified product. One certified smart deadbolt shows Thread and Bluetooth as its transport interfaces, with a certification date of May 1, 2025 (CSA, Philips Smart Lock listing). So look up the exact model you want. Two locks from one brand can connect differently.

Here is a simple test. For example, start with what you already own. Write down your phone and any smart home gear you own, then match it to the table. No hub at all? Pick Bluetooth or Wi-Fi. Already on a Thread system? A Matter lock makes sense.

Installing it yourself or hiring a locksmith

If the holes already exist, swapping a deadbolt is a decent weekend job. It goes sideways in three places.

  • No holes yet. Boring a new one takes a hole saw and a template. A crooked cut stays crooked.
  • A weak frame. Longer strike plate screws and a reinforced jamb protect you more than any feature on the lock.
  • A lock that won't pair. We test the connection at your door and leave it working. You don't fight an app at 9pm.

Frequently asked questions

Can any deadbolt be replaced with a smart lock?

Usually. The door needs standard holes and the right thickness, so measure first. A few smart locks also want extra room on the inside of the door, which catches people out.

Do smart locks work without Wi-Fi?

Keypad and Bluetooth locks do. What you lose is unlocking from across town. Typing a code or tapping your phone at the door still works fine.

What happens if the smart lock batteries die?

Most give a low battery warning long before it matters. Many keep a key cylinder too, or let you power the keypad from outside. Ask about that backup before you buy. Not after.

Does a deadbolt make a door secure by itself?

Not on its own. A great bolt in a weak frame still fails, because the bolt, the screws, the frame and the door all share the load. That's why we look at the frame first.
