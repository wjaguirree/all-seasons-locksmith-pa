Hardware Store, Kiosk or Locksmith for Copying a Key?

Hardware store, kiosk or locksmith for copying a key? It depends on the key. A plain brass house key? Walk it into a hardware store or feed it to a kiosk by the grocery checkout. Done in minutes. Car keys with a chip in the head are another story. So are worn-down originals and restricted keys, and those are the jobs where calling a mobile locksmith pays off. I'll lay the three options side by side below, then give you a quick check to run at the kitchen table before you choose.

Quick answer: A plain metal key in good shape is fine at a hardware store or kiosk. If it's a car key with a chip, a fob, a worn original or a restricted key, or you've run out of working keys, a locksmith is the better bet. What decides it is the key, not the shop.

Can a hardware store copy your key?

A hardware store is the usual first stop for an everyday house key, and for good reason. You hand over the original, and the person at the counter cuts a blank on a grinder. Per Wikipedia's overview of key duplication, as checked in October 2026, key duplication is available in many retail hardware stores, but the correct key blank may not be available. That second half matters. Counters stock the blanks they sell most, so an unusual key can come back as a near match that drags in the lock.

Two habits keep a counter copy honest. Ask for the exact blank, not a close one. Then test the copy in your lock before you leave the lot. A key that only turns with a jiggle becomes a stuck door on a cold January morning.

Do key kiosks work for every key?

Kiosks are fast. Insert the key, the machine reads it, and it cuts a matching blank while your cart waits. For a common residential key, that works fine. The limit is the blanks inside the machine. A 2012 review of one kiosk model by the security trade publication IPVM found it handled the most common residential and commercial blanks, but that the blanks "do not fit automobile, cabinet, or high-security lock profiles." Kiosks have changed since 2012, so read the supported-key list on the machine in front of you.

Key with a car maker's logo or a plastic head? Look for a clear yes on the screen before you pay.

When is a locksmith the right call?

A locksmith is the right call when the key does more than turn a lock. That includes most car keys. The Insurance Institute for Highway Safety reported in 2022 that immobilizers were standard on 96 percent of other manufacturers' 2015 vehicle models, with Hyundai and Kia at 26 percent. Drive something from the last decade or so? Odds are the key holds a chip your car wants to hear from before the engine will run. A counter can shape the blade. It can't teach the chip to introduce itself to your vehicle.

Call a locksmith when:

  • The key has a plastic head, buttons, or is a "smart" fob.
  • Only one key is left, and it's visibly worn.
  • The key is marked restricted or patented.
  • No working key remains, so there's nothing to copy from.
  • A past copy never worked right and you want it sorted out.

What is the copy of a copy problem?

The copy of a copy problem is the slow loss of accuracy when you duplicate a key that was itself a duplicate. Each copy picks up a small error from the machine and the blank. Copy the copy and the errors stack up. IPVM called this the kiosk's strongest weakness: since key copies are made from key copies, there is a significantly greater chance of cutting error. Wikipedia's overview adds that heavily worn keys cause trouble when a pattern grinder traces them. We've seen it on the road too. A spare that sticks is usually a worn copy of a worn key.

What helps is copying from the original or the best key you own, and retiring any spare that has started to stick. If a worn key is all you have, mention it before anyone starts cutting.

The Match the Key to the Maker method

Three steps, any key, any shop. We call it the Match the Key to the Maker method.

  1. Read the key. Plain metal, a blade with a plastic head, or a fob? Plastic head or buttons means a chip is likely.
  2. Choose the maker. Plain metal in good shape: counter or kiosk. Chip, fob, worn or restricted: locksmith.
  3. Test the copy. Before you leave, turn the key in the lock a few times. With a car key, lock the doors, unlock them and start the engine, because a key that clears only one of those three checks has failed.

If you'd rather change the lock than copy the key, lock rekeying resets the lock to a new key so every old copy stops working, which comes in handy after a move. Our post on rekeying after moving in covers that choice.

Frequently asked questions

Can Home Depot or Lowe's copy a car key?

Many big box stores cut the blade of a basic key. Chip programming is a separate step counters usually don't offer, so ask first.

Are key copy kiosks accurate?

For common residential keys in good shape, often yes. Accuracy slips when the original is worn or already a copy, because the machine copies what it sees.

How do I know if my car key has a chip?

A plastic head or push buttons usually means a chip, and so does a key for a car from the last decade or so. A plain all-metal key is less likely to have one. Unsure? Describe it to us by phone.

What if I have lost every key to my car?

Then there's nothing to copy, so it becomes a replacement. A mobile locksmith can make a new key on site. See our guide to car key replacement options.

Does the locksmith come to me?
